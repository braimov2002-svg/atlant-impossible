# CHAPTER 3. RESULTS OF SPECTRUM MODELLING AND DOSE-ENHANCEMENT CALCULATIONS

This chapter presents the results obtained with the developed model (DM) of Chapter 2 and with the reference code SpekPy v2.5.4 [@poludniowski2021; @spekpy_soft]. It begins with the validation of the DM (Section 3.1) and with the spectrum of the 45 kV copper-anode tube and its dependence on the design parameters and added filters (Sections 3.2–3.4). The dose enhancement factor (DEF) of Ag-loaded soft tissue and the optimisation of the filtration are analysed in Sections 3.5 and 3.6, the depth dependence, the tube voltage and the comparison with other high-Z elements in Sections 3.7 and 3.8, and the Monte Carlo verification, the uncertainty analysis and the discussion in Sections 3.9–3.11. Unless stated otherwise, the baseline conditions of {{REF:t2_1}} apply: a constant potential of 45 kV, an anode angle θ = 12°, a 0.5 mm Be window, 10 cm of air between the focal spot and the sample, and an energy bin width of 0.05 keV. The DEF is calculated in the kerma approximation, which assumes charged-particle equilibrium (CPE), for ICRU-44 soft tissue [@icru44] with the NIST mass energy-absorption coefficients [@nist_xraymass]. The ratio of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of Ag and tissue, $R(E)$, is defined as in Section 2.6.

## 3.1. Validation of the developed spectrum model

No measured spectrum of the particular tube was available, so the DM was validated against SpekPy v2, a deterministic physics-based toolkit that supports copper anodes at 10–50 kV [@poludniowski2021] and has been validated against measured spectra and half-value layers (HVL) [@bujila2020; @omar2020b]. The single adjustable parameter of the DM, the electron depth factor $\eta$ of {{REF:eq:depth}}, converts the path length in the continuous slowing-down approximation (CSDA) into the mean projected depth of the electrons. It was fitted to the shape of the bremsstrahlung continuum alone, with the characteristic lines switched off in both codes, for 45 kV, θ = 12° and 0.5 mm Be without the air path, by minimising the root-mean-square deviation of the area-normalised spectra,

$$
\delta(\eta) = \frac{1}{\max_i b_i}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(a_i(\eta) - b_i\right)^2\right]^{1/2}
$$ {#eq:rms}

where $a_i(\eta)$ and $b_i$ are the bremsstrahlung fluences of the DM and of SpekPy in energy bin $i$, each divided by its sum over the $N$ bins with $E_i > 3$ keV, and the factor $1/\max_i b_i$ expresses the deviation as a fraction of the spectrum maximum. The minimisation (Section 2.8) gave $\eta = 0.40$ with $\delta = 2.46$ %, against $\delta = 8.47$ % for straight-line penetration ($\eta = 1$). Because of multiple elastic scattering, the electrons thus generate photons at less than half the depth implied by a straight CSDA path: with the fitted value, the maximum projected depth of 45 keV electrons in copper is 2.536 µm, whereas their CSDA range is 6.481 µm. A shallower production depth shortens the exit path of the photons in the anode and reduces the self-absorption of the soft bremsstrahlung, which is why $\eta = 1$ yields too hard a spectrum. The value $\eta = 0.40$ was then kept fixed, so all other configurations are predictions rather than fits.

Results for tube voltages of 25–50 kV and anode angles of 6–30°, calculated without the air path, are collected in {{REF:t3_1}}. With 1 mm Al, which removes the Cu K lines, the first HVL ({{REF:eq:hvl}}) of the DM is lower than that of SpekPy by 3.5–4.6 %, and the mean energies differ by no more than 0.54 keV. Without added filtration the HVL deviation ranges from −2.6 % (45 kV, 6°) to −19.4 % (25 kV, 12°). These deviations originate in the characteristic radiation: the ratio of K-line to bremsstrahlung photons is 0.86–1.71 in the DM and 1.51–2.31 in SpekPy, i.e. the DM underestimates the Cu K lines by about 25–43 %. The deficit is largest at low voltage, where the Gryzinski cross-section [@gryzinski1965] rises too slowly above the ionisation threshold compared with the distorted-wave Born calculations [@bote2008; @llovet2014] underlying the K-line model of SpekPy [@omar2018]. A weaker 8 keV line gives more weight to the softer 3–8 keV continuum in the air kerma and thus lowers the first HVL of the unfiltered beam.

The spectral shapes for the baseline geometry with 10 cm of air are compared in {{REF:fig05_validation_cu}}. After normalisation to equal bremsstrahlung area above 9.6 keV, the two models agree within about 10 % between 10 and 30 keV, with and without 1 mm Al. The DM continuum is slightly richer in photons at 10–20 keV and poorer above about 25 keV, the ratio of the spectra falling to 0.80–0.85 at 40 keV; this mild softening explains the HVL deficit of about 4 % for filtered beams. Its probable causes are the non-relativistic Born cross-section with the Elwert factor [@koch1959], which only approximates the tabulated cross-sections [@seltzer1986], and the assumption of isotropic emission, whereas the model underlying SpekPy describes the energy and angular distribution of bremsstrahlung [@omar2020a; @poludniowski2021].

{{FIG:fig05_validation_cu|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air) calculated with SpekPy v2 and with the developed model, without added filtration (left) and with 1.0 mm Al (right). The spectra are normalised to equal bremsstrahlung area above 9.6 keV and scaled to the maximum of the SpekPy continuum; the lower panels show the ratio of the developed model to SpekPy in the bremsstrahlung region.}}

The absolute air kerma of the DM exceeds that of SpekPy by a factor of 1.08–1.22 without filtration and 1.21–1.42 with 1 mm Al. The main reason is the neglect of electron backscattering: the backscatter coefficient of copper is about 0.3, so a considerable fraction of the electrons leaves the anode before losing all their energy, whereas the DM assumes complete slowing-down of every electron; the approximate non-relativistic cross-section also contributes. Since the DEF depends only on the spectral shape, it is unaffected, and absolute dose rates are taken from SpekPy.

To check that the model is not tuned to copper only, the DM with the same $\eta$ was applied to a tungsten anode (θ = 12°) at 50, 80 and 100 kV with 2.5 mm Al, which removes the W L lines not included in the DM ({{REF:t3_1w}}). The HVL deviations are −5.7 %, −1.5 % and +0.5 %, and the mean energies of the DM are lower by about 1 keV. For orientation, the IEC 61267 radiation quality RQR3, a 50 kV tungsten beam, has a first HVL of 1.78 mm Al [@iec61267], comparable to both values obtained at 50 kV. As for copper, the tungsten spectra of the DM are slightly narrower than those of SpekPy, with a higher maximum and a weaker high-energy tail ({{REF:fig06_validation_w}}), and the agreement improves with voltage; one value of $\eta$ thus reproduces the HVLs of filtered beams within 6 % for anodes with Z = 29 and Z = 74. Overall, for filtered copper-anode beams the DM reproduces the HVL within about 4 %; its main weakness is the underestimated Cu K-line intensity, which matters for unfiltered beams and for beams dominated by Cu Kα. The main DEF results therefore use SpekPy spectra, with the DM as an independent check.

## 3.2. Spectrum of the 45 kV copper-anode tube

The spectrum of the tube under the baseline conditions is shown in {{REF:fig07_baseline}}. Its low-energy end is determined by the 0.5 mm Be window and the 10 cm air path, which together remove practically all photons below about 3 keV; the small kink near 3.2 keV is caused by the K-edge of the argon contained in air. Below the K-edge of copper the continuum rises to a broad plateau at about 7–9 keV and then drops abruptly at 8.98 keV: photons just above this energy are absorbed in the anode much more strongly than photons just below it, so that the anode acts as its own K-edge filter. Above the edge the continuum recovers to a shallow maximum near 12–13 keV and then decreases to zero at 45 keV, and the Ag K-edge falls into its weak high-energy part. The Cu Kα (8.05 keV) and Kβ (8.91 keV) lines rise far above the continuum; at the bin width of 0.05 keV the Kα peak exceeds the neighbouring continuum by more than two orders of magnitude. The DM reproduces this structure, with a continuum lying above that of SpekPy because of the higher absolute bremsstrahlung yield discussed in Section 3.1.

{{FIG:fig07_baseline|Spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air) at 10 cm from the focal spot calculated with SpekPy v2 and with the developed model. The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge (25.514 keV).}}

The characteristic radiation and the beam-quality parameters are summarised in {{REF:t3_2}}. According to SpekPy, characteristic photons make up 68.5 % of the photon fluence, so the unfiltered beam is essentially a source of Cu K radiation with an admixture of bremsstrahlung. In the DM the line intensities follow from 0.0159 K-shell ionisations per incident 45 keV electron, the fluorescence yield of copper $\omega_K = 0.4538$ and the relative radiative rates 0.583 (Kα1, 8.048 keV), 0.299 (Kα2, 8.028 keV) and 0.118 (Kβ1,3, 8.905 keV) from xraylib [@schoonjans2011]; indirect fluorescence, excited by bremsstrahlung absorbed in the anode, contributes 4.5 % of the Kα and 4.8 % of the Kβ intensity. In the SpekPy spectrum the Kβ/Kα fluence ratio, integrated over narrow energy windows around the lines, is 0.1431. It slightly exceeds the ratio of the radiative rates (0.118/0.882 = 0.134), because the more energetic Kβ photons, which still lie below the Cu K-edge, are attenuated less in the anode, the window and air, and because the windows also contain some continuum. A 20 µm Ni filter, whose K-edge (8.333 keV, {{REF:t2_2}}) lies between the Kα and Kβ energies, reduces this ratio to 0.0023; this is the classical β-filter of x-ray diffraction [@cullity2001].

The quality of the unfiltered beam is very low. SpekPy gives a mean energy $\bar{E}$ ({{REF:eq:emean}}) of 10.38 keV, a first HVL of 0.0478 mm Al, a second HVL of 0.0563 mm Al, a homogeneity coefficient of 0.85 and an effective energy of 7.82 keV; the corresponding DM values are 10.74 keV, 0.0464 mm Al, 0.0570 mm Al, 0.81 and 7.74 keV. An HVL below 50 µm Al and an effective energy below the Kα energy show that the air kerma, and hence the dose to soft tissue, is delivered mainly by the Cu K lines and the soft continuum. Only 4.64 % of the photons (DM: 5.04 %) lie above the Ag K-edge, carrying 14.1 % of the energy fluence (DM: 14.7 %), and their share in the kerma is smaller still because $\mu_{en}/\rho$ decreases steeply with photon energy. The output is very high: the air kerma at 10 cm is 383 mGy/mAs according to SpekPy (423 mGy/mAs in the DM), which corresponds to 15.3 Gy/s at 40 mA, and the photon fluence is $3.66 \times 10^{10}$ cm$^{-2}$ per mAs. Combined with the dominance of 8 keV photons, for which $R(E)$ is only 21.0, this predicts a modest DEF for the bare beam, as quantified in Section 3.5.

## 3.3. Influence of tube design parameters

Three design parameters were varied one at a time around the baseline: the anode angle, the tube voltage and the thickness of the Be window. The spectra are shown in {{REF:fig08_angle}}, {{REF:fig09_kvp}} and {{REF:fig10_be}}, and the beam-quality parameters, the air kerma and the DEF for 1 % Ag are listed in {{REF:t3_3}}.

Increasing the anode angle from 6° to 30° raises the air kerma from 296 to 463 mGy/mAs, i.e. by a factor of 1.56, while the mean energy decreases from 10.73 to 10.07 keV and the HVL from 0.0497 to 0.0455 mm Al. The reason is self-filtration in the anode. A photon produced at depth $d$ leaves the anode along the central ray over a path $d/\sin\theta$, which at 6° is about 4.8 times longer than at 30°. As {{REF:fig08_angle}} shows, the effect is strongest where copper is most opaque, at the soft end of the spectrum and just above the Cu K-edge, where the fluence at small angles is reduced several-fold. It is weaker between about 6 keV and the edge, and above about 25 keV, where copper is much more transparent, the curves nearly merge. The same mechanism causes the heel effect, the decrease of intensity towards the anode side of the radiation field [@bushberg2012]; the present calculations refer to the central ray only. A small anode angle thus gives a smaller effective focal spot but a lower output and a slightly harder beam, while the DEF for 1 % Ag changes only from 1.2035 at 6° to 1.1991 at 30°.

{{FIG:fig08_angle|Influence of the anode angle (6–30°) on the spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The tube voltage has the strongest influence on the output. When the voltage rises from 25 to 50 kV, the air kerma increases from 163 to 430 mGy/mAs, the mean energy from 8.93 to 10.75 keV and the HVL from 0.0402 to 0.0487 mm Al. The increase in output comes largely from the Cu K lines: at higher voltage the electrons travel a longer path at energies where the K-shell ionisation cross-section is close to its maximum of about 351 b, reached near 35 keV. {{REF:fig09_kvp}} shows that the continuum extends up to the maximum photon energy $eU$, where $e$ is the elementary charge and $U$ the tube voltage, and that its high-energy part grows strongly, whereas the fluence just above the Cu K-edge is almost independent of the voltage. Photons of this energy are absorbed in copper so strongly that only those produced in a thin layer below the anode surface escape, while the additional bremsstrahlung that faster electrons produce at greater depths is reabsorbed in the anode. The DEF increases only from 1.1932 at 25 kV to 1.2027 at 50 kV. At 25 kV no photon reaches the Ag K-edge, yet the DEF is already 1.19: the enhancement of the unfiltered beam is therefore caused almost entirely by photoabsorption in the L and M shells of Ag below its K-edge, where $R(E)$ is 21–32.

{{FIG:fig09_kvp|Spectra of the Cu-anode tube at tube voltages of 25–50 kV (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The Be window affects only the soft end of the spectrum ({{REF:fig10_be}}). Beryllium (Z = 4) absorbs weakly, so increasing the window thickness from 0.15 to 2.0 mm removes mainly photons below about 6 keV; the Cu K lines and the continuum near 8 keV are weakened by a smaller fraction, and the spectrum above 10 keV hardly changes. The air kerma decreases from 423 to 271 mGy/mAs, while the mean energy rises from 10.23 to 10.99 keV, the HVL from 0.0454 to 0.0528 mm Al and the DEF from 1.1989 to 1.2061. Together with the air path, the window sets the low-energy cut-off of the beam reaching the sample. Its thickness is fixed by the tube design rather than chosen by the user, but it must be known accurately for spectrum calculations, since it changes the output by more than one third over the studied range.

{{FIG:fig10_be|Influence of the Be window thickness (0.15–2.0 mm) on the low-energy part of the spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (θ = 12°, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2).}}

The DEF values obtained with the DM spectra agree with those of SpekPy within 0.003 for all design variants ({{REF:t3_3}}). Overall, the design parameters change the output by factors of up to 1.6 (anode angle, window) and 2.6 (voltage), but the DEF for 1 % Ag remains between 1.19 and 1.21. In every unfiltered beam the tissue dose is dominated by the Cu K lines and by photons of 3–15 keV, for which $R(E)$ is only 21–27, and the design parameters shift the balance between these components only slightly. The tube design is therefore a lever for the dose rate rather than for the DEF; a substantial increase in the DEF requires reshaping the spectrum with added filters.

## 3.4. Influence of added filters on the spectrum

Added filters reshape the spectrum through the energy-dependent transmission $\exp[-\mu_f(E)\,t]$ of {{REF:eq:filter}}, where $\mu_f(E)$ is the linear attenuation coefficient of the filter material and $t$ is its thickness; SpekPy applies the same law with its own attenuation data. Three groups of filters were studied: aluminium, whose K-edge (1.56 keV) lies below the spectrum, so that its attenuation decreases smoothly with energy; nickel and copper, whose K-edges lie near the Cu K lines; and molybdenum, silver and tin, whose K-edges (20.0, 25.514 and 29.2 keV, {{REF:t2_2}}) lie in the hard part of the spectrum. The spectra are shown in {{REF:fig11_al}}, {{REF:fig12_cu_ni}} and {{REF:fig13_kedge}}, and the beam parameters are given in {{REF:t3_4}}.

Aluminium filters act as classical hardening filters ({{REF:fig11_al}}). In this energy range the attenuation in aluminium is dominated by photoelectric absorption, whose cross-section falls roughly as $E^{-3}$, so the soft continuum and the Cu K lines are removed far more efficiently than the photons above the Ag K-edge. With 0.25 mm Al the Kα line is weakened by more than an order of magnitude but remains visible, and with 1–2 mm Al it practically disappears. The maximum of the continuum moves from below 9 keV without a filter to about 27 keV with 2 mm Al, while the fluence above 30 keV decreases only moderately. Correspondingly, for 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 mm Al the mean energy increases to 19.73, 23.78, 26.13 and 28.59 keV, and the fraction of photons above the Ag K-edge to 25.8, 38.2, 49.8 and 65.0 %. The HVL rises abruptly from 0.104 mm Al with the 0.25 mm filter to 0.454 mm Al with the 0.5 mm filter, because behind the thinner filter the residual Cu K lines still carry a large part of the air kerma and therefore determine the first HVL. The price is the output: the relative air kerma $K/K_0$ is 0.0588, 0.0173, 0.00829 and 0.00372, so 1 mm Al reduces the dose rate about 120-fold and 2 mm Al about 270-fold.

{{FIG:fig11_al|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube with added Al filters of 0.25–2.0 mm (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

Copper filters behave differently ({{REF:fig12_cu_ni}}). The K-edge of copper (8.979 keV) lies just above its own Kα and Kβ energies, so a copper filter is relatively transparent to the Cu K radiation while absorbing the continuum at 9–20 keV very strongly. A 0.05 mm Cu filter therefore gives $\bar{E}$ = 15.37 keV but an HVL of only 0.0575 mm Al. With 0.1 mm Cu the mean energy (24.08 keV) is close to that obtained with 0.5 mm Al (23.78 keV), yet the HVL (0.0702 mm Al) is 6.5 times smaller. The two quantities are weighted differently: the mean energy is weighted by fluence and reflects the hard continuum, whereas the HVL is weighted by air kerma and is controlled by the transmitted Kα line, for which $\mu_{en}/\rho$ of air is much larger. Only 0.2 mm Cu suppresses the lines sufficiently; this beam has $\bar{E}$ = 33.09 keV, an HVL of 1.403 mm Al and 91.2 % of the photons above the Ag K-edge, but a relative air kerma of only $7.64 \times 10^{-4}$. A 0.02 mm Ni filter, with its K-edge between Kα and Kβ, suppresses the Kβ line and the continuum just above 8.33 keV, which appears as a step in the spectrum, while transmitting Kα with a moderate loss; the air kerma falls to 0.339 of its unfiltered value. The result is a quasi-monochromatic 8 keV beam ($\bar{E}$ = 12.09 keV, 10.3 % of photons above the Ag K-edge), suitable for x-ray diffraction [@cullity2001] but not for dose enhancement by Ag.

{{FIG:fig12_cu_ni|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube with a 0.02 mm Ni filter and with Cu filters of 0.05–0.2 mm (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The K-edge filters of Mo, Ag and Sn are compared with 1 mm Al in {{REF:fig13_kedge}}. Each of them is relatively transparent just below its K-edge and opaque just above it, so it transmits a band below the edge and cuts the fluence above the edge several-fold or more; narrow residual Cu K lines remain visible behind the Mo and thin Sn filters because of their high initial intensity. The 0.05 mm Mo filter transmits the 12–20 keV band, where its spectrum lies above the Al-filtered one, but strongly suppresses the photons above 20 keV, so that only 31.5 % of its photons exceed the Ag K-edge. The 0.05 mm Ag filter follows the Al-filtered curve up to 25.5 keV and then removes precisely the photons that are most useful for enhancement by Ag. Although its HVL (0.624 mm Al) is comparable to that of 0.05 mm Sn (0.606 mm Al) and larger than that of 0.5 mm Al (0.454 mm Al), only 20.1 % of its photons lie above the Ag K-edge, against 34.3 % and 38.2 %. Tin preserves the 25.5–29.2 keV window directly above the Ag K-edge but removes the photons above 29.2 keV, where $R(E)$ is highest (102.7 at 30 keV and 138.8 at 45 keV). Consequently, 0.1 mm Sn gives a slightly larger HVL than 1 mm Al (0.902 against 0.830 mm Al) but a smaller fraction of photons above the Ag K-edge (40.4 % against 49.8 %).

{{FIG:fig13_kedge|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube filtered by K-edge absorbers (Mo 0.05 mm, Ag 0.05 mm, Sn 0.05 and 0.1 mm) compared with the spectrum filtered by 1.0 mm Al, relative to the maximum of the Al-filtered spectrum (SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

These results show that neither the mean energy nor the HVL alone characterises the part of the spectrum that matters for Ag. The DEF values in {{REF:t3_4}} broadly follow the fraction of photons above the Ag K-edge as long as the kerma is not dominated by the Cu K lines: the 0.1 mm Cu filter, with 55.1 % of the photons above the edge, gives a DEF of only 1.288, because the transmitted Kα line delivers most of the tissue dose. The DEF is analysed in detail in the next section.

## 3.5. Dose enhancement factor of Ag-loaded soft tissue

In the kerma approximation the DEF is the ratio of two spectrum-weighted integrals ({{REF:eq:def_spec}}), so its value is determined by the distribution of the product $E\,\Phi(E)\,\mu_{en}/\rho$ over energy. This dose integrand is shown in {{REF:fig14_integrand}} for ICRU-44 tissue and for tissue containing 1 % Ag by mass. Without added filtration the integrand is dominated by the Cu K lines and by the continuum at 3–15 keV, and above about 20 keV it falls below $10^{-4}$ of its maximum: although 14.1 % of the energy fluence lies above the Ag K-edge, $\mu_{en}/\rho$ of tissue is so small there that these photons contribute almost nothing to the dose. In the dominant region $R(E)$ is only 21.0 at 8 keV, 23.0 at 10 keV and 26.9 at 15 keV, so 1 % Ag raises the integrand almost uniformly by about 20 %. With 1 mm Al the integrand is concentrated between 15 and 45 keV with a maximum near 18–19 keV, and the residual Kβ line exceeds the Kα line because aluminium attenuates the lower-energy line more strongly. At the Ag K-edge the integrand of the loaded tissue jumps, whereas that of pure tissue is continuous. Above the edge $R(E)$ increases from 76.2 at 25.6 keV to 102.7 at 30 keV and 138.8 at 45 keV, so the gap between the two curves widens; since about half of the photons of this beam lie above the edge, this region provides the larger part of the enhancement.

{{FIG:fig14_integrand|Energy-resolved dose integrand, the product of photon energy, fluence spectrum and mass energy-absorption coefficient, for ICRU-44 soft tissue (shaded) and for tissue containing 1 % Ag (line), without added filtration (left) and with 1.0 mm Al (right); 45 kV Cu-anode tube, SpekPy v2. The values are relative to the maximum of the tissue integrand; the dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The DEF increases linearly with the Ag mass fraction $w$ for every filter ({{REF:fig15_def_conc}}, {{REF:t3_5}}). The linearity follows from the mixture rule for $\mu_{en}/\rho$: according to {{REF:eq:def_lin}}, $\mathrm{DEF} = 1 + w(\langle R\rangle - 1)$, where $\langle R\rangle$ is the mean of $R(E)$ weighted by the dose spectrum of unloaded tissue ({{REF:eq:ratio_avg}}). The slope $(\mathrm{DEF}-1)/w$ per 1 % Ag is 0.202 without a filter, 0.377 with 0.5 mm Al, 0.482 with 1 mm Al, 0.623 with 2 mm Al and 0.804 with 0.2 mm Cu, which corresponds to $\langle R\rangle$ of about 21, 39, 49, 63 and 81. For 1 % Ag the DEF is 1.20 without a filter, 1.48 with 1 mm Al, 1.62 with 2 mm Al and 1.80 with 0.2 mm Cu; for 2 % Ag the corresponding values are 1.40, 1.96, 2.25 and 2.61. At 0.1 % Ag (1 mg of Ag per gram of loaded tissue) the DEF lies between 1.020 and 1.080, an enhancement of only 2–8 %, whereas at 0.5 % it reaches 1.241 with 1 mm Al. The strict linearity holds at the surface of the sample; inside an extended Ag-loaded region the attenuation by Ag itself makes the dependence non-linear (Section 3.7).

{{FIG:fig15_def_conc|Dose enhancement factor of ICRU-44 soft tissue as a function of the Ag mass fraction for different added filters (45 kV Cu-anode tube, SpekPy v2 spectra, kerma approximation at the surface).}}

The ranking of the filters confirms that the DEF is governed by the share of the dose integrand above the Ag K-edge rather than by the mean energy. For 1 % Ag the highest values are obtained with 0.2 mm Cu (1.804) and 2 mm Al (1.623), followed by 0.2 mm Sn (1.524) and 1 mm Al (1.482). The Ag and Mo filters (1.342 and 1.337) remain below 0.5 mm Al (1.377), and the thin Cu and Ni filters (1.208–1.288) improve only slightly on the unfiltered beam. The Ni-filtered beam, being nearly a pure Cu Kα source, gives practically the same DEF as the unfiltered beam (1.208 against 1.202). At a similar HVL, 0.1 mm Sn yields a lower DEF than 1 mm Al (1.444 against 1.482), because it removes the hardest photons.

The last two columns of {{REF:t3_4}} compare the DEF obtained with the two spectrum models. Without filtration they practically coincide (1.2012 for the DM against 1.2015 for SpekPy) and differ by less than 0.3 % for all design variants ({{REF:t3_3}}). With 0.5–2 mm Al the DM is lower by 0.6–1.3 % (for example, 1.4638 against 1.4819 for 1 mm Al), and for the Sn, Ag and Mo filters the deviation is below 1 %. The sign agrees with Section 3.1: the weaker high-energy tail of the DM continuum reduces the weight of the photons above the Ag K-edge. Where residual Cu K lines carry a substantial part of the kerma, the DM gives higher values, by 0.5 % for 0.25 mm Al and by about 2 % for 0.1 and 0.2 mm Cu (1.3107 against 1.2876 and 1.8433 against 1.8041), because the underestimated lines shift the dose towards the hard continuum. Differences of 1–2 % are small compared with the differences between filters, so the DEF is robust to the choice of spectrum model.

The choice of the reference medium has a small but systematic effect. With water instead of ICRU-44 soft tissue, the DEF for 1 % Ag is 1.2018 without a filter, 1.4965 with 1 mm Al, 1.644 with 2 mm Al and 1.8274 with 0.2 mm Cu, i.e. up to 1.3 % higher for filtered beams. Although ICRU-44 tissue contains less oxygen than water, its 1.3 % by mass of Na, P, S, Cl and K, elements of higher atomic number, increases its photoabsorption, so that its $\mu_{en}/\rho$ exceeds that of water at 15–40 keV and the DEF is lower. This difference should be taken into account when DEF values calculated for water are compared with values for tissue.

## 3.6. Optimisation of the filtration

Every filter raises the DEF at the expense of the dose rate, so the optimal filter maximises the DEF at a given transmission. {{REF:fig16_tradeoff}} shows the DEF for 1 % Ag versus the relative air kerma for seven filter materials of increasing thickness (xraylib attenuation coefficients applied to the unfiltered SpekPy spectrum). The Al and Ti curves practically coincide and lie highest from 1 down to about $10^{-3}$, whereas the Ni and Cu curves stay low while the Cu Kα line is transmitted and rise steeply only below about $10^{-2}$. The K-edge filters fall behind: the Sn curve lies below the Al and Ti curves at all transmissions, the Mo curve levels off near 1.4, and the Ag curve passes through a maximum of about 1.35 and then decreases.

{{FIG:fig16_tradeoff|Dose enhancement factor for 1 % Ag versus the relative air kerma (transmission) of the beam for filters of Al, Ti, Ni, Cu, Mo, Ag and Sn of increasing thickness (45 kV Cu-anode tube, θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2).}}

For a systematic comparison, the thickness $t$ of each filter was found from the condition of equal air-kerma transmission

$$
\mathcal{T}_K(t) = \frac{\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi_0(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_{\mathrm{air}}\exp\left[-\mu_f(E)\,t\right]\mathrm{d}E}{\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi_0(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_{\mathrm{air}}\,\mathrm{d}E}
$$ {#eq:transm}

where $\mathcal{T}_K(t)$ is the air-kerma transmission, $\Phi_0(E)$ is the fluence spectrum of the unfiltered beam, $(\mu_{en}/\rho)_{\mathrm{air}}$ is the mass energy-absorption coefficient of air, $\mu_f(E)$ is the linear attenuation coefficient of the filter element and $T_0$ = 45 keV is the maximum photon energy. Equation {{REF:eq:transm}} was solved numerically (Section 2.8) for $\mathcal{T}_K$ = 10 %, 1 % and 0.1 % for the elements from Al (Z = 13) to Bi (Z = 83), excluding gases, liquids, P, S, Se, the alkali metals, radioactive elements, Tl and the lanthanides other than Gd. The results are shown in {{REF:fig17_filter_scan}} and, for selected elements, in {{REF:t3_6}}.

The dependence on Z reflects the positions of the absorption edges of the filter relative to the Cu lines and to the Ag K-edge; at 10 % transmission all elements still give 1.220–1.241. Elements from Al to Co (Z = 13–27), whose K-edges lie below the Cu Kα energy, absorb the Cu lines strongly, attenuate the rest of the spectrum smoothly and give 1.88–1.91 at 0.1 %. At 1 % the DEF decreases from Ti to Co (1.452 to 1.397), because a K-edge close to the Kα energy lets more of the soft continuum below the edge leak through. For Ni to As (Z = 28–33) the K-edge lies above the Kα energy, so the line is transmitted and dominates the kerma, making these the worst filters at 10 % and 1 %.

Elements from Sr to Ag (Z = 38–47), with K-edges between the Cu lines and the Ag K-edge (20.0 keV for Mo), absorb the photons above the Ag K-edge and at 0.1 % form a deep minimum, lowest for Ag (1.335), Pd (1.340) and Rh (1.346). For Cd to Ba (Z = 48–56) the K-edges lie above the Ag K-edge, and the DEF at 0.1 % grows with Z (1.529 for Sn, 1.692 for I). Gd and heavier elements, whose K-edges exceed 45 keV, act through L-shell absorption: Gd, with its L3 edge (7.243 keV) below the Cu Kα energy, behaves like the light absorbers (1.883 at 0.1 %), whereas for Hf to Bi the L3 edges lie above Kα (11.919 keV for Au, {{REF:t2_2}}), the line leaks through, and W, Au and Pb give only 1.350–1.351 at 1 %.

{{FIG:fig17_filter_scan|Dose enhancement factor for 1 % Ag obtained with filters of different elements (Z = 13–83) whose thickness gives an equal air-kerma transmission of 10 %, 1 % and 0.1 % (45 kV Cu-anode tube, θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2).}}

At 10 % and 1 % transmission the light and medium-Z absorbers Al, Si, Ca, Sc and Ti give practically the same DEF (1.236–1.241 and 1.452–1.455, respectively); at 0.1 % Ca, Sc, Ti and V give 1.907, whereas Al gives 1.884, i.e. 1.2 % less. As calcium and scandium are chemically or technically unsuitable as filter foils, Al and Ti are the practical materials. Titanium needs a much thinner foil (111.2 instead of 824.4 µm at 1 %) and is slightly more effective at strong attenuation, because in the several-millimetre-thick Al filter the weakly energy-dependent Compton scattering contributes more to the attenuation of the hard photons.

For an irradiation experiment, {{REF:t3_7}} gives the tissue kerma at the sample surface and the time needed to deliver a dose $D$ at tube current $I$,

$$
t_D = \frac{D}{c\,I\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_t\,\mathrm{d}E}
$$ {#eq:texp}

where $D$ = 2 Gy, $I$ = 40 mA, $\Phi(E)$ is the fluence spectrum per unit tube charge at 10 cm (cm$^{-2}$ keV$^{-1}$ mAs$^{-1}$), $(\mu_{en}/\rho)_t$ refers to ICRU-44 tissue and $c = 1.602 \times 10^{-13}$ Gy g keV$^{-1}$ converts keV/g into Gy; under CPE this tissue kerma equals the absorbed dose at the surface, without backscatter (Section 2.5). Without a filter 2 Gy are delivered in 0.12 s (16.5 Gy/s), which is difficult to time reproducibly, whereas with 1 mm Al (0.137 Gy/s) and 2 mm Al (0.062 Gy/s) the irradiation takes 14.6 and 32.5 s. The 0.2 mm Cu filter gives the highest DEF of the studied set (1.80) only because of its very strong attenuation (at an equal transmission of 0.1 %, Cu gives 1.74, Al and Ti 1.88–1.91). It needs 158.4 s, and near this thickness the DEF changes steeply ({{REF:fig16_tradeoff}}), which makes it sensitive to thickness tolerances.

{{TABLE:t3_7}}

Filtration is thus the decisive design lever for dose enhancement by Ag with a copper-anode tube. Aluminium of 1–2 mm, which raises the DEF for 1 % Ag from 1.20 to 1.48–1.62 at convenient exposure times, or titanium of 0.1–0.3 mm is the optimal choice. K-edge filters with edges at 20–30 keV (Mo, Ag, Sn) and filters that transmit the Cu Kα line (Ni, thin Cu) should be avoided.
