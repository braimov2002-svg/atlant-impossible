# INTRODUCTION

**Relevance of the topic.** Cancer is one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide [@bray2024], and radiotherapy is one of its main treatment methods: about half of all cancer patients have an indication for it [@delaney2005]. The effectiveness of radiotherapy is limited by the tolerance of the healthy tissues surrounding the tumour [@hall2018; @baskar2012]. One of the strategies to optimise the dose delivered in external radiotherapy is therefore the use of radiosensitizers, agents that increase the effect of radiation on the tumour more than on normal tissue [@wardman2007]. Among them, compounds and nanoparticles of heavy elements are now studied intensively [@gill2019; @kuncic2018].

The physical basis of radiosensitization by heavy elements is the photoelectric effect. Its cross-section per unit mass increases approximately as $Z^4$ and decreases approximately as $E^{-3}$, so at kilovoltage photon energies elements of high atomic number $Z$ absorb radiation tens to hundreds of times more strongly than the light elements of soft tissue [@podgorsak2016]. A tumour containing such an element therefore receives a higher dose than the surrounding tissue. Combined with 250 kVp (kilovolt peak) x-rays, gold nanoparticles markedly prolonged the survival of tumour-bearing mice [@hainfeld2004], and Monte Carlo (MC) calculations showed that the dose enhancement by gold is much larger for kilovoltage than for megavoltage beams [@cho2005]. Inexpensive and widely available kilovoltage x-ray tubes are thus natural photon sources for photon activation therapy with heavy elements, as calculations for gadolinium compounds show [@abdullaeva2022].

Silver is a promising alternative to gold. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) were proposed as a radiosensitizer for glioma [@liu2013] and outperformed gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) in vitro and in mice [@liu2016]; silver is also cheaper than gold. Most importantly for the choice of the beam, the K-edge of silver at 25.514 keV lies inside the spectrum of any x-ray tube operated above about 26 kV, whereas the K-edge of gold (80.725 keV) requires much harder beams. Above its K-edge silver absorbs energy per unit mass 76–139 times more strongly than soft tissue, against only 21–32 times between 8 keV and the edge (Chapter 1). Comparison with samarium ($Z$ = 62), whose K-edge (46.83 keV) lies just above the maximum photon energy of a 45 kV tube, shows how the position of the absorption edges determines the dose enhancement [@abdullaeva2024].

The physical radio-enhancement is quantified by the dose enhancement factor (DEF), the ratio of the absorbed doses in tissue with and without the heavy element. Since the absorption ratio changes several-fold at the K-edge, the DEF depends on the fraction of the photon energy fluence above the edge and thus on the whole spectrum of the beam [@cho2005; @roeske2007]. Kilovoltage beams, however, are usually specified only by the tube voltage and the half-value layer (HVL) [@ma2001], which do not fix the spectrum around the edge, and data on the spectra of x-ray tubes are often missing. The spectrum must therefore be calculated from the design and technical parameters of the tube [@poludniowski2021]. Most spectrum models have been developed for tungsten anodes [@boone1997; @poludniowski2009], whereas copper-anode tubes serve mainly x-ray diffraction [@cullity2001]. The unfiltered beam of a 45 kV copper-anode tube consists mainly of the Cu K lines at 8.0–8.9 keV, far below the Ag K-edge; these photons are absorbed superficially and contribute little to the enhancement. The filtration that shifts the spectrum above the Ag K-edge, and the loss of dose rate it causes, have to be determined by modelling.

The problem is also of direct practical importance. The compact REIS-I x-ray generator with a 45 kV BS1 tube, available in the laboratory, has been adapted for experiments on photon activation therapy (Section 2.10). Planning irradiations of silver-containing biological samples with this facility requires knowledge of its spectrum, of the filtration that maximises the DEF at an acceptable exposure time and of the radiation-safety conditions for the personnel. The modelling of the spectrum of a 45 kV copper-anode x-ray tube on the basis of its design, technical parameters and filters, and the calculation of the DEF of biological matter containing silver, are therefore a relevant problem of medical physics.

**Degree of study of the problem.** Dose enhancement near high-Z media goes back to Spiers's analysis of energy absorption near irradiated bone [@spiers1949]; iodine enhancement was later calculated for kilovoltage beams [@mello1983; @solberg1992] and used to treat brain metastases with a modified computed tomography scanner [@rose1999]. Gold nanoparticles have been studied in cells and by MC simulation [@chithrani2010; @rahman2009; @zhang2009; @leung2011], their mechanisms reviewed [@butterworth2012; @her2017; @schuemann2016] and elements compared across photon energies [@roeske2007; @mcmahon2016]; the enhancement around nanoparticles is highly localised [@jones2010], which can explain biological effects beyond the macroscopic enhancement [@mcmahon2011]. Radiosensitization by AgNPs was shown in cancer cells [@xu2009], glioma [@liu2018; @zhao2019], breast cancer cells [@swanner2015] and colorectal cancer [@habiba2019]. AgNPs are themselves cytotoxic and genotoxic, inducing reactive oxygen species [@asharani2009], so part of their effect may be chemical or biological. Silver dosimetry was studied by MC for brachytherapy [@taha2019], tissues around a loaded tumour [@caglar2024] and skin [@esitmez2026] and in dose gels [@merkis2023]; depth-dependent enhancement in orthovoltage beams was examined in an MC phantom [@chow2023].

X-ray spectrum models range from empirical [@boone1997] and semi-empirical [@birch1979; @tucker1991] to deterministic physics-based [@poludniowski2007a; @poludniowski2007b; @poludniowski2009] and MC models [@verhaegen1999; @ay2004], mostly for tungsten anodes; SpekPy, validated against measurements [@bujila2020], supports a copper anode in version 2 [@poludniowski2021]. The analytical models go back to Kramers [@kramers1923] and to the Bethe–Heitler cross-section [@bethe1934], and Kramers' model was later extended to thick targets [@omar2020a]. The research group calculated the dose enhancement by gadolinium compounds [@abdullaeva2022] and modelled the spectrum of a 45 kVp tube for radio-enhancement with Ag and Sm [@abdullaeva2024]. However, the dependence of the spectrum of a low-voltage copper-anode tube on its design and filtration, and of the resulting DEF of silver-containing tissue, has been studied insufficiently.

**Aim of the work.** The aim is to model the spectrum of a 45 kV x-ray tube with a copper anode on the basis of its design and technical parameters and filters, and to calculate with this spectrum the DEF of biological matter (soft tissue) containing silver.

**Tasks of the research.** To achieve this aim, the following tasks were solved:

1. to review radiosensitization by high-Z elements, the interaction of kilovoltage photons with biological matter and the methods of modelling x-ray tube spectra;
2. to develop a physical model of the spectrum of a copper-anode x-ray tube (electron slowing-down, bremsstrahlung, characteristic radiation, self-absorption and filtration) and methods of calculating the air kerma, beam quality and DEF;
3. to analyse the cross-sections of photon interaction with silver and the mass attenuation coefficients of soft tissue, brain, bone, water and silver;
4. to describe the x-ray irradiation facility based on the REIS-I generator and the radiation-safety requirements for work with a kilovoltage x-ray tube;
5. to calculate the spectra of a 45 kVp tube with a 100 µm copper transmission anode without a filter and with aluminium filters;
6. to analyse the K- and L-edge characteristics of the elements with $Z$ = 30–65 and the energy dependence of the ratios of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of Ag and Sm to that of soft tissue;
7. to calculate the DEF of soft tissue containing Ag and Sm as a function of the element mass fraction and the filtration.

**Object of the research.** The object is the x-ray radiation of a 45 kV copper-anode tube and its interaction with silver-containing soft tissue.

**Subject of the research.** The subject is the formation of the spectrum, its dependence on the tube design and filtration, and the resulting DEF.

**Research methods.** The spectrum is calculated with the developed model (DM), a semi-analytical thick-target model based on the Bethe stopping power, the non-relativistic Bethe–Heitler cross-section with the Elwert factor and the Gryziński K-shell ionisation cross-section [@bethe1930; @bethe1934; @elwert1939; @gryzinski1965], with SpekPy v2 as the reference [@poludniowski2021], and with the Kramers formulas for a transmission anode [@kramers1923] together with the Kanaya–Okayama electron range [@kanaya1972]. Photon interaction data are taken from the XCOM database and the x-ray mass attenuation tables of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) [@nist_xcom; @nist_xraymass] and atomic data from xraylib [@schoonjans2011], with the ICRU-44 soft tissue of the International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements (ICRU) as the reference medium [@icru44]. The DEF is calculated in the kerma approximation [@attix1986]. The calculations use Python with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib [@harris2020; @virtanen2020; @hunter2007].

**Scientific novelty.** The novelty of the work is as follows:

1. A semi-analytical model of the spectrum of a low-voltage copper-anode x-ray tube was developed. It takes into account electron slowing-down in the anode, bremsstrahlung with the Elwert correction, direct and bremsstrahlung-induced K radiation, self-absorption in the anode and attenuation in the window, air and filters, with a single parameter fitted to SpekPy.
2. For a 45 kVp tube with a 100 µm copper transmission anode it was shown that the self-filtration of the anode suppresses the photons between the Cu K-edge and about 14 keV, and that a 2.11 mm aluminium filter removes the Cu K lines and concentrates the spectrum above the silver K-edge.
3. It was established that the DEF of soft tissue containing silver increases linearly with its mass fraction and that thick aluminium filtration raises the DEF for 1 % Ag from 1.40 to 1.98. With thin filtration samarium is more effective than silver, owing to its L-edges just below the Cu K lines, whereas with thick filtration silver is more effective, because its K-edge, unlike that of samarium, lies inside the spectrum.

**Practical significance.** The results can be used to choose the filtration and exposure conditions for radiobiological experiments with silver-containing radiosensitizers on the REIS-I facility and other kilovoltage x-ray tubes. The model allows the spectrum, air kerma and beam quality of low-voltage copper-anode tubes to be calculated from their design and technical parameters when measured spectra are not available. The analysis of the absorption edges shows how the radiosensitizer and the filter should be selected jointly: for silver, filtration that removes the photons below its K-edge (about 2 mm of aluminium) should be used. The radiation-safety analysis gives the measures required for work with the facility.

**Reliability of the results.** Reliability is ensured by the use of evaluated data: the NIST photon cross-sections and attenuation and energy-absorption coefficients [@nist_xcom; @nist_xraymass], the stopping-power parameters of ICRU Report 37 [@icru37] and the xraylib atomic data [@schoonjans2011]. The Bethe stopping power reproduces the NIST ESTAR data for water [@nist_estar], and the spectrum of the DM reproduces the bremsstrahlung shape of SpekPy v2 at 45 kV with a root-mean-square deviation of 2.46 % [@poludniowski2021]. The pair-production cross-section of silver with complete screening agrees with the XCOM data within 4 %, and the calculated DEF values are consistent with the energy dependence of the mass energy-absorption coefficients.
