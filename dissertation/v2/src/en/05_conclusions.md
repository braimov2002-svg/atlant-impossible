# CONCLUSIONS

1. High-Z dose enhancement is much larger for kilovoltage than for megavoltage photons, and silver nanoparticles were reported to radiosensitize glioma, breast and colorectal cancer models. The Ag/tissue ratio of mass energy-absorption coefficients rises from 21–32 below the Ag K-edge (25.514 keV) to 103–139 at 30–45 keV, so the dose enhancement by silver is determined by the tube spectrum and its filtration.

2. The developed model (DM) is a semi-analytical thick-target model of the copper-anode tube spectrum (Bethe stopping power, non-relativistic Bethe–Heitler cross-section with the Elwert factor, Gryziński K-shell cross-section) that includes bremsstrahlung-induced K fluorescence, anode self-absorption and filtration. Its single parameter, the electron depth factor $\eta$ = 0.40, was fitted to the SpekPy v2 bremsstrahlung shape at 45 kV with a root-mean-square deviation of 2.46 %.

3. Under charged-particle equilibrium the DEF is the spectrum-weighted ratio of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of the loaded and unloaded tissue. At the surface $\mathrm{DEF} - 1$ is proportional to the silver mass fraction, with a slope determined by the share of the dose spectrum above the Ag K-edge; inside the tissue the DEF increases with depth owing to beam hardening and decreases in a heavily loaded region owing to self-shielding.

4. In silver the photoelectric effect dominates up to 0.24 MeV, so in the spectrum of a 45 kV tube the interaction is almost purely photoelectric, with a 5.8-fold rise of the attenuation coefficient at the K-edge. Water, soft tissue and brain have practically equal mass attenuation coefficients at 1–100 keV, cortical bone attenuates 3.5–5.3 times more strongly at 10–30 keV, and silver 97–108 times more strongly than soft tissue between its K-edge and 30 keV.

5. The unfiltered 45 kV copper-anode tube is a very intense source (15.3 Gy/s at 10 cm and 40 mA) that requires shielding, interlocks and dosimetric control. The REIS-I facility with a BS1 tube (45 kV, 100 µA) in a lead shielding chamber with a 1-mm lead cover, which transmits at most 6×10⁻⁶ of the primary photons, provides safe irradiation of biological samples.

6. The spectrum of a 45 kVp tube with a 100 µm copper transmission anode consists of the Cu K lines (8.05 and 8.91 keV) and a bremsstrahlung continuum at 15–45 keV with a maximum at about 27–28 keV; the self-filtration of the anode suppresses the photons between 9 and 14 keV. A 2.11 mm aluminium filter removes the Cu K lines and the photons below about 20 keV and concentrates the spectrum above the Ag K-edge, whereas a 30 µm filter changes the spectrum only slightly.

7. The K-shell binding energies of the elements with $Z$ = 30–65 lie between 9.7 and 52 keV, and the absorption increases 4.8–7.2-fold at the K-edge (5.8-fold for silver), 2.6–4.7-fold at the L3 edge and less than 1.5-fold at the L1 and L2 edges. The K-edge of silver (25.5 keV) lies inside the spectrum of the 45 kVp tube, and that of samarium (46.8 keV) above it.

8. The DEF of soft tissue grows linearly with the mass fraction of the element. For 1 % of silver it is 1.40 with the 30 µm Al filter and 1.98 with the 2.11 mm Al filter (5.88 for 5 % of silver). With thin filtration samarium is more effective than silver (DEF 1.56 for 1 %), because its L-edges lie just below the Cu K lines; with thick filtration silver is more effective than samarium (1.98 against 1.81). Filtration is the decisive parameter for dose enhancement by silver.

9. The results show the possibility of radiosensitization with silver and kilovoltage x-ray tubes. The cascades of Auger electrons and fluorescence photons after inner-shell ionisation make the microscopic dose distribution strongly inhomogeneous, which must be taken into account in dosimetry and in estimating the biological effectiveness.

## Practical recommendations

1. For irradiation of silver-containing samples with a 45 kV copper-anode tube, use thick aluminium filtration (about 2 mm, e.g. 2.11 mm Al) that removes the Cu K lines and the photons below about 20 keV; avoid unfiltered and thinly filtered beams.
2. Specify the beam (voltage, anode, window, filters and measured first HVL) and report the DEF together with the spectrum and the reference medium.
3. Plan exposure times from the calculated tissue kerma rate for the chosen filter, taking into account the lower dose rate of thick filters, and verify the output dosimetrically.
4. Operate the REIS-I generator only in the shielding chamber with the 1-mm lead cover; keep the personnel at least 1.5 m behind or beside the high-voltage unit and carry out dose-rate surveys.
5. Choose the filter jointly with the radiosensitizer: for elements whose K-edge lies inside the spectrum, such as silver, hard filtration is advantageous, whereas for elements whose K-edge lies above the maximum photon energy, such as samarium, thin filtration may be preferable.
