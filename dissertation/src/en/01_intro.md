# INTRODUCTION

**Relevance of the topic.** Cancer is a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [@bray2024], and about half of all cancer patients have an indication for radiotherapy (RT) [@delaney2005; @baskar2012]. The effectiveness of anticancer RT is still limited by the tolerance of the healthy tissues that surround the tumour and inevitably receive part of the dose [@hall2018; @baskar2012]. The therapeutic window can be widened either by improving the conformity of the dose distribution or by increasing the response of the tumour to a given dose. The second strategy relies on radiosensitizers, that is, agents that enhance the effect of radiation in the tissue where they accumulate [@hall2018; @wardman2007]. Besides chemical radiosensitizers [@wardman2007], compounds and nanoparticles of heavy metals are now studied intensively [@gill2019; @kuncic2018].

Elements with a high atomic number $Z$ absorb kilovoltage photons much more strongly than soft tissue, because the photoelectric cross-section increases steeply with $Z$ and decreases with photon energy [@podgorsak2016]. A tumour loaded with such an element therefore absorbs more energy per unit mass than the surrounding tissue. Gold nanoparticles 1.9 nm in diameter markedly improved the one-year survival of tumour-bearing mice irradiated with 250 kVp (kilovolt peak) x-rays [@hainfeld2004], and Monte Carlo (MC) calculations showed that the macroscopic dose enhancement by gold is much larger for kilovoltage than for megavoltage beams [@cho2005]. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) were proposed as a radiation sensitizer for glioma [@liu2013] and were reported to outperform gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) in radiosensitizing glioma cells in vitro and in an intracranial mouse model [@liu2016]. Silver is also much cheaper than gold, and its K absorption edge at 25.514 keV lies inside the energy range of low-voltage x-ray tubes.

The physical effect of silver is quantified by the dose enhancement factor (DEF), the ratio of the absorbed dose in tissue containing silver to that in the same tissue without it. The ratio of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of silver and soft tissue is only 21–32 below the Ag K-edge but 100–139 above it, so the DEF is determined mainly by the fraction of the energy fluence above 25.514 keV; in general, dose enhancement depends strongly on photon energy [@cho2005; @roeske2007]. Kilovoltage beams are usually specified by the tube voltage and the half-value layer (HVL) [@ma2001], but these two parameters do not determine the spectral distribution near an absorption edge. A reliable DEF calculation therefore requires the spectrum of the x-ray source itself.

This work considers an x-ray tube with a copper anode operated at 45 kV. Unlike the tungsten-anode tubes used in diagnostic radiology and orthovoltage therapy, it emits most of the photons of the unfiltered beam in the Cu K lines at 8.0–8.9 keV, far below the Ag K-edge, together with a bremsstrahlung continuum that extends up to 45 keV. The DEF obtained with such a tube depends on its design and technical parameters (anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window) and on the filters placed in the beam. Modelling the spectrum of a copper-anode tube as a function of these parameters, and using it to determine the DEF of biological matter containing silver, is therefore a relevant problem of medical physics.

**Degree of study of the problem.** The concept of dose enhancement near high-Z media goes back to Spiers, who analysed the increased energy absorption in and near bone irradiated with kilovoltage x-rays [@spiers1949]. Dose enhancement by iodine was calculated for kilovoltage beams [@mello1983; @solberg1992] and used in the first radiotherapy of human brain metastases delivered with a modified computed tomography scanner [@rose1999]. Since the work of Hainfeld et al. [@hainfeld2004], gold nanoparticles have been studied extensively in cells and by MC simulation [@chithrani2010; @rahman2009; @zhang2009; @leung2011], and their mechanisms have been summarised in several reviews [@butterworth2012; @her2017; @schuemann2016; @kuncic2018]. Different elements were compared theoretically as functions of photon energy [@roeske2007; @mcmahon2016]. It was also shown that the dose enhancement around nanoparticles is highly localised [@jones2010] and that such nanoscale energy deposition can explain biological effects beyond the macroscopic dose enhancement [@mcmahon2011].

For silver, an early report showed that AgNPs sensitize cancer cells to radiation-induced killing [@xu2009], and later studies demonstrated radiosensitization by AgNPs in glioma [@liu2013; @liu2016; @liu2018; @zhao2019], in breast cancer cells [@swanner2015] and in colorectal cancer [@habiba2019]. AgNPs are themselves cytotoxic and genotoxic and induce reactive oxygen species (ROS) and DNA damage [@asharani2009], so part of their effect may be chemical or biological rather than physical. Dosimetric studies of silver include MC calculations for brain-tumour brachytherapy [@taha2019], for the healthy tissues surrounding a loaded tumour [@caglar2024] and for layered skin geometries in comparison with gold [@esitmez2026], as well as measurements in AgNP-enriched dose gels [@merkis2023]. The depth dependence of nanoparticle dose enhancement in orthovoltage beams has also been studied by MC simulation [@chow2023].

Methods of modelling x-ray tube spectra range from empirical [@boone1997] and semi-empirical models [@birch1979; @tucker1991] to physics-based deterministic models [@poludniowski2007a; @poludniowski2007b; @poludniowski2009; @poludniowski2021] and full MC simulation [@verhaegen1999; @ay2004]. Most of them were developed for tungsten anodes [@boone1997; @tucker1991; @poludniowski2009], whereas the SpekPy v2 toolkit, validated against measured spectra and HVLs [@bujila2020], also supports a copper anode [@poludniowski2021]. Copper-anode tubes are used mainly in x-ray diffraction, where a nearly monochromatic Cu Kα beam is obtained with a nickel filter [@cullity2001], a purpose opposite to dose enhancement by silver. Thus, the relation between the design and filtration of a low-voltage copper-anode tube, its spectrum and the DEF of silver-loaded tissue has not been the subject of a dedicated quantitative study in the literature reviewed in this work; this gap determined the aim and tasks of the dissertation.

**Aim of the work.** The aim of the work is to model the spectrum of a 45 kV copper-anode x-ray tube as a function of its design and technical parameters and filtration, and on this basis to determine quantitatively the DEF of soft tissue containing silver and the filtration that maximises it at an acceptable dose rate.

**Tasks of the research.** To achieve this aim, the following tasks were solved:

1. To analyse the literature on radiosensitization by high-Z elements, in particular silver, and on the modelling of x-ray tube spectra.
2. To develop a semi-analytical thick-target model of the spectrum of a copper-anode tube that includes electron slowing-down, bremsstrahlung, direct and bremsstrahlung-induced characteristic K radiation, self-absorption in the anode and attenuation in the window, air and filters.
3. To validate the developed model against the SpekPy v2 toolkit and to determine its single adjustable parameter.
4. To calculate the spectrum and beam-quality parameters of the 45 kV tube and their dependence on the anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window and added filters.
5. To calculate the DEF of soft tissue containing 0.1–2 % silver by mass and to find the filters that maximise it at an acceptable dose rate.
6. To study the depth dependence of the DEF and the self-shielding by a silver-loaded region, and to verify the analytical results by an independent MC calculation.
7. To compare silver with other high-Z elements, to estimate the uncertainty of the DEF and to formulate practical recommendations for experiments.

**Object of the research.** The object of the research is the x-ray radiation of a 45 kV x-ray tube with a copper anode and its interaction with soft biological tissue containing silver.

**Subject of the research.** The subject of the research is the formation of the x-ray spectrum, its dependence on the tube design and filtration, and the resulting DEF of silver-loaded tissue.

**Research methods.** The spectrum is calculated with a semi-analytical thick-target model developed in this work (the developed model, DM), which is based on the Bethe stopping power in the continuous-slowing-down approximation (CSDA), the non-relativistic Bethe–Heitler bremsstrahlung cross-section with the Elwert factor and the Gryzinski K-shell ionisation cross-section [@bethe1930; @bethe1934; @elwert1939; @gryzinski1965]; SpekPy v2.5.4 serves as the reference model [@poludniowski2021; @spekpy_soft]. Interaction data are taken from the NIST tables of Hubbell and Seltzer [@nist_xraymass] and from the xraylib library [@schoonjans2011], with ICRU-44 soft tissue as the reference medium [@icru44]. The DEF is calculated in the kerma approximation, which assumes charged-particle equilibrium (CPE) [@attix1986], and is verified by an MC photon-transport code written for this work that uses Woodcock tracking [@woodcock1965] and models Ag K fluorescence and coherent and incoherent scattering [@hubbell1975]. The uncertainty is estimated by a sensitivity analysis; the calculations are implemented in Python with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib [@harris2020; @virtanen2020; @hunter2007].

**Scientific novelty.** The scientific novelty of the work consists in the following:

1. A semi-analytical model of the spectrum of a reflection-type copper-anode x-ray tube has been developed with a single adjustable parameter, the electron depth factor $\eta$, and with the bremsstrahlung-induced characteristic K radiation described by a closed-form expression. With $\eta = 0.40$, the DM reproduces the HVL of the SpekPy v2 spectra of filtered beams within 3.5–4.6 % and their mean energy within 0.54 keV for 25–50 kV and anode angles of 6–30°.
2. It has been established that, without added filtration, the DEF for 1 % silver is only 1.20 and stays within 1.19–1.21 for anode angles of 6–30°, tube voltages of 25–50 kV and beryllium windows of 0.15–2 mm, because the dose is dominated by the Cu K lines and soft bremsstrahlung. Added filtration is the decisive factor: the DEF is 1.48 with 1 mm Al, 1.62 with 2 mm Al and 1.80 with 0.2 mm Cu.
3. At equal air-kerma transmission, light and medium-Z filters without absorption edges in the 9–45 keV range (Al, Si, Ca, Sc, Ti) give the highest DEF, whereas filters with K-edges at 20–30 keV (Mo, Ag, Sn) are the least effective at strong filtration. At 0.1 % transmission, the DEF for 1 % silver is about 1.91 with Sc or Ti filters but only 1.34 with an Ag filter.
4. With 1 mm Al filtration, the DEF for 1 % silver increases from 1.48 at the surface to 1.69 at a depth of 20 mm (beam hardening) but decreases to 1.21 at 10 mm inside a uniformly loaded region (self-shielding); the dose behind a 10 mm layer containing 1 % silver falls to about 0.80 of the silver-free value. An independent MC calculation for this layer confirmed the analytical model within 1–10 % and revealed the role of reabsorbed Ag K fluorescence and scattered photons.
5. Per unit mass, silver is a weaker physical sensitizer at 45 kV than gold, platinum, bismuth and gadolinium (DEF 1.48 against 2.16 for gold with 1 mm Al filtration) and is comparable to iodine (1.46). The stronger biological radiosensitization reported for AgNPs than for AuNPs [@liu2016] therefore points to chemical and biological mechanisms beyond the macroscopic physical dose enhancement.

**Practical significance.** The DM and its software allow the spectrum, air kerma and beam quality of low-voltage copper-anode tubes to be calculated for different design parameters and filters, which is needed to plan radiobiological experiments with silver-containing radiosensitizers. For a 45 kV copper-anode tube, an aluminium filter 1–2 mm thick (or a titanium filter 0.1–0.3 mm thick) is recommended. With aluminium, the DEF for 1 % silver is 1.48–1.62, while a surface tissue kerma of 2 Gy at 10 cm and 40 mA is still delivered in 15–33 s. It was also found that the CS_Energy function of xraylib 4.3.0 underestimates the mass energy-absorption coefficients of light elements by up to 37 % (oxygen at 30 keV), which would overestimate the DEF (1.72 instead of 1.48); the NIST tables should therefore be used in such calculations.

**Reliability of the results.** The reliability of the results is ensured by established physical models and evaluated data: the NIST photon interaction tables [@nist_xraymass] and the stopping-power parameters of ICRU Report 37 [@icru37]. The implemented Bethe formula reproduces the NIST ESTAR stopping power of water at 50 keV (6.603 MeV cm$^2$/g) [@nist_estar]. The DM agrees with SpekPy v2, which has been validated against measured spectra and HVLs [@bujila2020], also for a tungsten anode at 50–100 kV (within 6 % in HVL). The energy-transfer coefficients reconstructed from the interaction data of the MC code reproduce the NIST mass energy-absorption coefficients of H, C and O at 20–40 keV within 0.3 %, and the MC and analytical DEF values agree within 1–10 %. The sensitivity analysis gives DEF = 1.48 ± 0.02 ($k = 1$) for 1 % silver and 1 mm Al filtration.
