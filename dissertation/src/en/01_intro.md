# INTRODUCTION

**Relevance of the topic.** Cancer is a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [@bray2024], and about half of all cancer patients have an indication for radiotherapy (RT) [@delaney2005], whose effectiveness is still limited by the tolerance of the surrounding healthy tissues [@hall2018; @baskar2012]. The therapeutic window can be widened by increasing the tumour response with radiosensitizers, agents that enhance the effect of radiation where they accumulate [@hall2018; @wardman2007]; besides chemical agents, compounds and nanoparticles of heavy metals are now studied intensively [@gill2019; @kuncic2018]. Elements with a high atomic number $Z$ absorb kilovoltage photons much more strongly than soft tissue, because the photoelectric cross-section rises steeply with $Z$ and falls with photon energy [@podgorsak2016]. Gold nanoparticles markedly improved the survival of tumour-bearing mice irradiated with 250 kVp (kilovolt peak) x-rays [@hainfeld2004], and the dose enhancement by gold is much larger for kilovoltage than for megavoltage beams [@cho2005]. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) were proposed as a radiosensitizer for glioma [@liu2013] and reported to outperform gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) in vitro and in an intracranial mouse model [@liu2016]; silver is also less expensive, and its K absorption edge at 25.514 keV lies inside the spectrum of a 45 kV x-ray tube.

The physical effect of silver is quantified by the dose enhancement factor (DEF), the ratio of the absorbed doses in tissue with and without silver. The ratio of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of silver and soft tissue is only 21–32 at 8–25 keV, jumps to about 76 at the Ag K-edge and rises to 100–139 at 30–45 keV, so the DEF depends strongly on the fraction of the photon energy fluence above the edge [@cho2005; @roeske2007]. The tube voltage and half-value layer (HVL) that usually specify kilovoltage beams [@ma2001] do not determine the spectrum around an absorption edge; a reliable DEF calculation therefore requires the source spectrum itself, as provided by spectrum models [@poludniowski2021]. Unlike the tungsten-anode tubes of radiology and orthovoltage therapy [@bushberg2012; @khan2014], a 45 kV copper-anode tube emits most photons of the unfiltered beam in the Cu K lines at 8.0–8.9 keV, far below the Ag K-edge, together with bremsstrahlung up to 45 keV. Its spectrum, and hence the DEF, is governed by the anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window and added filters, so modelling it and determining the DEF of silver-containing biological matter is a relevant problem of medical physics.

**Degree of study of the problem.** The concept of dose enhancement near high-Z media goes back to Spiers's analysis of energy absorption in and near bone irradiated with kilovoltage x-rays [@spiers1949]. Dose enhancement by iodine was calculated for kilovoltage beams [@mello1983; @solberg1992] and used in the first radiotherapy of brain metastases with a modified computed tomography scanner [@rose1999]. Gold nanoparticles have since been studied extensively in cells and by Monte Carlo (MC) simulation [@chithrani2010; @rahman2009; @zhang2009; @leung2011], their mechanisms have been reviewed [@butterworth2012; @her2017; @schuemann2016], and elements have been compared as functions of photon energy [@roeske2007; @mcmahon2016]. The dose enhancement around nanoparticles is highly localised [@jones2010], and nanoscale energy deposition can explain biological effects beyond the macroscopic dose enhancement [@mcmahon2011]. For silver, AgNPs were shown to sensitize cancer cells to radiation-induced killing [@xu2009], and radiosensitization was demonstrated in glioma [@liu2018; @zhao2019], breast cancer cells [@swanner2015] and colorectal cancer [@habiba2019]. AgNPs are themselves cytotoxic and induce reactive oxygen species and DNA damage [@asharani2009], so part of their effect may be chemical or biological rather than physical. Dosimetric studies of silver include MC calculations for brain-tumour brachytherapy [@taha2019], healthy tissues around a loaded tumour [@caglar2024] and layered skin geometries [@esitmez2026], measurements in AgNP-enriched dose gels [@merkis2023] and an MC phantom study of the depth dependence of dose enhancement in orthovoltage beams [@chow2023].

Spectrum models range from empirical [@boone1997] and semi-empirical [@birch1979; @tucker1991] to physics-based deterministic [@poludniowski2007a; @poludniowski2007b; @poludniowski2009] and MC models [@verhaegen1999; @ay2004], most of them developed for tungsten anodes. The SpekPy toolkit, validated against measured spectra and HVLs [@bujila2020], supports a copper anode in version 2 [@poludniowski2021]. Copper-anode tubes are used mainly in x-ray diffraction, where a nickel filter yields a nearly monochromatic Cu Kα beam [@cullity2001], whereas dose enhancement by silver requires photons above the Ag K-edge. The relation between the design and filtration of such a tube, its spectrum and the DEF of silver-loaded tissue has therefore not been studied quantitatively in the literature reviewed, which determined the aim and tasks of this work.

**Aim of the work.** The aim is to model the spectrum of a 45 kV copper-anode x-ray tube as a function of its design and technical parameters and filtration and, on this basis, to determine the DEF of soft tissue containing silver and the filtration that maximises it at an acceptable dose rate.

**Tasks of the research.** To achieve this aim, the following tasks were solved:

1. To review the literature on radiosensitization by high-Z elements, in particular silver, and on x-ray spectrum modelling.
2. To develop a semi-analytical thick-target spectrum model of a copper-anode tube including electron slowing-down, bremsstrahlung, direct and bremsstrahlung-induced characteristic K radiation, self-absorption in the anode and attenuation in the window, air and filters.
3. To validate the model against SpekPy v2 and determine its single adjustable parameter.
4. To calculate the spectrum and beam-quality parameters of the 45 kV tube as functions of the anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window and added filters.
5. To calculate the DEF of soft tissue containing 0.1–2 % silver by mass and find the filters that maximise it at an acceptable dose rate.
6. To study the depth dependence of the DEF and the self-shielding by a silver-loaded region, and to verify the results by an independent MC calculation.
7. To compare silver with other high-Z elements, estimate the uncertainty of the DEF and formulate practical recommendations for experiments.

**Object of the research.** The object is the x-ray radiation of a 45 kV copper-anode tube and its interaction with soft biological tissue containing silver.

**Subject of the research.** The subject is the formation of the x-ray spectrum, its dependence on the tube design and filtration, and the resulting DEF of silver-loaded tissue.

**Research methods.** The spectrum is calculated with the developed model (DM), a semi-analytical thick-target model based on the Bethe collision stopping power in the continuous-slowing-down approximation, the non-relativistic Bethe–Heitler bremsstrahlung cross-section with the Elwert factor and the Gryzinski K-shell ionisation cross-section [@bethe1930; @bethe1934; @elwert1939; @koch1959; @gryzinski1965]; SpekPy v2.5.4 serves as the reference model [@spekpy_soft]. Interaction data are taken from the NIST tables [@nist_xraymass] and the xraylib library [@schoonjans2011], with ICRU-44 soft tissue as the reference medium [@icru44]. The DEF is calculated in the kerma approximation, which assumes charged-particle equilibrium [@attix1986], and verified by an MC photon-transport code written for this work that uses Woodcock tracking [@woodcock1965], models Ag K fluorescence and coherent and incoherent scattering [@hubbell1975] and deposits the energy of secondary electrons locally. The uncertainty is estimated by a sensitivity analysis; the calculations are implemented in Python with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib [@harris2020; @virtanen2020; @hunter2007].

**Scientific novelty.** The scientific novelty of the work consists in the following:

1. A semi-analytical spectrum model of a reflection-type copper-anode tube has been developed with a single adjustable parameter, the electron depth factor $\eta$, and a closed-form expression for the bremsstrahlung-induced characteristic K radiation. With $\eta = 0.40$, fitted to the SpekPy v2 bremsstrahlung shape (root-mean-square deviation 2.46 %), the DM reproduces without refitting the HVL of SpekPy spectra filtered by 1 mm Al within −3.5 to −4.6 % and their mean energy within 0.54 keV for 25–50 kV and anode angles of 6–30°.
2. Without added filtration, the DEF for 1 % silver by mass is only 1.20 and stays within 1.19–1.21 for anode angles of 6–30°, tube voltages of 25–50 kV and beryllium windows of 0.15–2 mm, because the dose is dominated by the Cu K lines and soft bremsstrahlung. Filtration is the decisive factor: the DEF is 1.48 with 1 mm Al, 1.62 with 2 mm Al and 1.80 with 0.2 mm Cu, at the cost of a 120–1300-fold lower air kerma.
3. At equal air-kerma transmission, light and medium-Z filters without absorption edges at 9–45 keV (Al, Si, Ca, Sc, Ti) give the highest DEF, whereas filters with K-edges at 20–30 keV (Mo, Ag, Sn) are the least effective at strong filtration: at 0.1 % transmission, the DEF for 1 % silver is about 1.91 with Sc or Ti but only 1.34 with Ag.
4. With 1 mm Al filtration, the DEF for 1 % silver rises from 1.48 at the surface to 1.69 at a depth of 20 mm (beam hardening) but falls to 1.21 at 10 mm inside a uniformly loaded region (self-shielding), and the dose behind a 10 mm layer containing 1 % silver drops to about 0.80 of the silver-free value. An independent MC calculation confirmed the analytical model within 1–10 % and revealed the role of reabsorbed Ag K fluorescence and scattered photons.
5. Per unit mass, silver is a weaker physical sensitizer at 45 kV than gold, platinum, bismuth and gadolinium (DEF 1.48 against 2.16 for gold with 1 mm Al) and comparable to iodine (1.46), so the stronger biological radiosensitization reported for AgNPs than for AuNPs [@liu2016] points to chemical and biological mechanisms and nanoscale effects.

**Practical significance.** The DM and its software calculate the spectrum, air kerma and beam quality of low-voltage copper-anode tubes for given design parameters and filters, as needed for planning radiobiological experiments with silver-containing radiosensitizers. For a 45 kV tube, a 1–2 mm aluminium filter (or a 0.1–0.3 mm titanium filter) is recommended; with aluminium, a tissue kerma of 2 Gy at the sample surface (10 cm from the focal spot, 40 mA) is delivered in about 15–33 s. The CS_Energy function of xraylib 4.3.0 was found to underestimate the mass energy-absorption coefficients of light elements at 15–45 keV by up to 37 % (oxygen at 30 keV), which would overestimate the DEF (1.72 instead of 1.48 for 1 % silver and 1 mm Al); the NIST tables should therefore be used.

**Reliability of the results.** Reliability is ensured by evaluated data, namely the NIST photon interaction tables [@nist_xraymass] and the stopping-power parameters of ICRU Report 37 [@icru37], and by independent checks. The implemented Bethe formula reproduces the NIST ESTAR collision stopping power of water at 50 keV (6.603 MeV cm$^2$/g) [@nist_estar], and the DM reproduces the SpekPy HVL for a tungsten anode at 50–100 kV within 6 %, SpekPy itself having been validated against measurements [@bujila2020]. The energy-transfer coefficients of the MC code reproduce the NIST mass energy-absorption coefficients of H, C and O at 20–40 keV within 0.3 %. The sensitivity analysis gives DEF = 1.48 ± 0.02 (coverage factor $k = 1$) for 1 % silver and 1 mm Al filtration.
