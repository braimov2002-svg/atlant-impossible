## 1.5. Silver-based nano-radiosensitizers

Silver ($Z = 47$) lies between the light elements of soft tissue and the heavy elements, such as gold ($Z = 79$), that dominate the literature on high-Z radiosensitizers. Reviews cover the synthesis, properties and therapeutic applications of silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) [@wei2015] and their antimicrobial and anticancer uses [@burdusel2018]; AgNPs are cheaper than gold and can be made by green synthesis, for example in egg white [@lu2012]. For radiotherapy, two properties of silver matter: its biological activity and the position of its K-edge within kilovoltage tube spectra. The former is linked to toxicity: AgNPs are cytotoxic and genotoxic in human cells, inducing ROS, DNA damage and cell-cycle arrest with mitochondrial involvement [@asharani2009]. Such effects belong to the chemical and biological stages of radio-enhancement (Section 1.4) [@kuncic2018; @rosa2017], so AgNPs may sensitize cells beyond their physical dose enhancement. Their cytotoxicity, however, makes selective tumour accumulation a prerequisite of therapy, and the effect of dose enhancement on healthy tissues must be assessed [@caglar2024].

Nano-sensitizers are classified by their main element into noble-metal (Au, Ag, Pt), lanthanide (Gd, Hf), other heavy-metal (Bi, W) and non-metal materials, and by their mechanism into physical (increased energy absorption), chemical (catalytic production of ROS) and biological (cell-cycle redistribution, inhibition of DNA repair, induction of autophagy and apoptosis) sensitizers [@zhang2022; @liu2018t]. Their efficacy depends on size, shape, surface coating, concentration and location as well as on the photon energy [@zhang2022]; for gold, for example, nanotriangles radiosensitized glioblastoma cells and tumours in mice [@bhattarai2017]. Silver nanoparticles combine a moderately high atomic number with a pronounced chemical activity: polyvinylpyrrolidone-coated AgNPs activated autophagy through elevated ROS rather than through released silver ions [@zhu2017], gallic-acid-coated AgNPs suppressed radiation-induced epithelial–mesenchymal transition in lung cancer cells [@sunilgowda2018], and conjugation with the aptamer AS1411 and verapamil increased the accumulation of AgNPs in glioma cells and their radiosensitizing effect [@zhao2021].

Experimental evidence is summarised in {{REF:tab:lit_ag}}: since the early report by Xu et al. [@xu2009], glioma has been the most studied model [@liu2013; @liu2016; @liu2018; @zhao2019], including radioresistant hypoxic cells [@hall2018]; breast [@swanner2015] and colorectal [@habiba2019] cancers have also been used. The studies differ in particles, coatings, biological models and irradiation conditions and are not quantitatively comparable. Because the physical dose enhancement depends strongly on the spectrum (Section 1.4), an enhancement ratio reported without the spectrum cannot be split into physical and non-physical parts.

The reported advantage of AgNPs over AuNPs [@liu2016] is instructive. Per unit mass, silver and gold absorb photon energy comparably below about 12 keV, but gold absorbs more strongly between about 12 and 45 keV ({{REF:fig03_ratio}}), and in megavoltage beams the macroscopic enhancement is much smaller even for gold [@cho2005] because Compton scattering, whose mass coefficient depends only weakly on $Z$, dominates [@podgorsak2016]. A larger macroscopic physical dose enhancement is thus unlikely to explain the advantage of silver, either below 45 keV or at megavoltage energies. This points to substantial contributions from the chemical and biological activity of silver [@asharani2009] and from nanoscale energy deposition [@mcmahon2011]; confirming this requires quantifying the physical part for a well-defined beam.

Dosimetric studies of silver are fewer than biological ones: MC simulations addressed AgNPs in a brain tumour during brachytherapy [@taha2019], surrounding healthy tissues [@caglar2024] and silver versus gold in layered skin [@esitmez2026]; dose enhancement was also measured in AgNP-enriched dose gels [@merkis2023], and its depth dependence in orthovoltage beams was studied for nanoparticles [@chow2023]. A systematic analysis of how kilovoltage tube design and filtration determine the DEF of silver-loaded tissue has not been found in the literature reviewed.

The choice of beam follows from the atomic data of silver ({{REF:t2_2}}): K-edge 25.514 keV, L3 edge 3.351 keV and Kα1 line 22.163 keV. With $\omega_K = 0.831$, only about 17 % of K vacancies relax by Auger transitions that deposit their energy locally [@krause1979; @bambynek1972]; the other 83 % emit mainly Kα photons that carry their energy away (Section 2.1.1). Nevertheless, the silver-to-tissue ratio of $\mu_{en}/\rho$ is 76–139 above the edge, against 21–32 between 8 keV and the edge (Sections 2.2 and 1.4). Unlike the gold K-edge (80.725 keV), usable only above about 81 kV, the silver K-edge lies inside the spectrum of any tube operated above about 26 kV. The most favourable spectra therefore carry much of their energy fluence between the Ag K-edge and the end-point energy (45 keV here), far above the Cu K lines at 8.0–8.9 keV that dominate the photon fluence of the unfiltered copper-anode beam.

## 1.6. Conclusions to Chapter 1

1. Ionising radiation damages biological matter in physical, physico-chemical, chemical and biological stages; DNA, damaged directly or by water radicals (about two thirds of the damage for x-rays), is the main target, with mitochondria as a second target. Clustered damage and DSBs, misrepaired by non-homologous end joining, lead to cell death, mutations and chromosomal aberrations; radiosensitizers act by increasing the damage or impairing its repair.
2. The physical dose enhancement by a high-Z element in kilovoltage beams is governed by its mass energy-absorption coefficient relative to that of tissue, which for silver rises sharply above the K-edge at 25.514 keV; the physical efficacy of silver is thus set mainly by the fraction of the energy fluence above this edge, and a DEF is meaningful only together with its spectrum.
3. Silver nanoparticles radiosensitize glioma, breast and colorectal cancer models, in one study more effectively than gold nanoparticles, and act also chemically and biologically through ROS, autophagy and impaired repair. The macroscopic physical contribution of silver for a well-characterised kilovoltage beam, needed to separate physical from chemical and biological effects, has not been determined, and spectrum models have been developed mainly for tungsten anodes. This work therefore models the 45 kV copper-anode tube spectrum and calculates the DEF of silver-loaded tissue versus tube design, filtration, concentration, depth and medium.
