# CHAPTER 3. RESULTS OF SPECTRUM MODELLING AND DOSE-ENHANCEMENT CALCULATIONS

The results were obtained with the developed model (DM) of Chapter 2 and with the reference code SpekPy v2.5.4 [@poludniowski2021; @spekpy_soft]. Unless stated otherwise, the baseline conditions of {{REF:t2_1}} apply: a constant potential of 45 kV, an anode angle θ = 12°, a 0.5 mm Be window, 10 cm of air between the focal spot and the sample, and 0.05 keV energy bins. The dose enhancement factor (DEF) is calculated in the kerma approximation, which assumes charged-particle equilibrium (CPE), for the ICRU-44 soft tissue of the International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements (ICRU) [@icru44] with the mass energy-absorption coefficients of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) [@nist_xraymass]; $R(E)$ is the ratio of these coefficients for Ag and tissue (Section 2.6).

## 3.1. Validation of the developed spectrum model

No measured spectrum of the particular tube was available, so the DM was validated against SpekPy v2, a deterministic physics-based code that supports copper anodes at 10–50 kV [@poludniowski2021] and has been validated against measured spectra and half-value layers (HVL) [@bujila2020; @omar2020b]. The only adjustable parameter of the DM, the electron depth factor $\eta$ of {{REF:eq:depth}}, was fitted to the bremsstrahlung continuum alone (lines switched off in both codes; 45 kV, θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, no air) by minimising the root-mean-square deviation of the area-normalised spectra,

$$
\delta(\eta) = \frac{1}{\max_i b_i}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(a_i(\eta) - b_i\right)^2\right]^{1/2}
$$ {#eq:rms}

where $a_i(\eta)$ and $b_i$ are the bremsstrahlung fluences of the DM and of SpekPy in energy bin $i$, each divided by its sum over the $N$ bins with $E_i > 3$ keV, and the factor $1/\max_i b_i$ expresses the deviation as a fraction of the spectrum maximum. The fit gave $\eta = 0.40$ with $\delta = 2.46$ %, against $\delta = 8.47$ % for straight-line penetration ($\eta = 1$). Because of multiple elastic scattering, photons are thus produced at less than half the depth of a straight path in the continuous slowing-down approximation (CSDA), 2.536 µm instead of 6.481 µm for 45 keV electrons in copper, and the soft bremsstrahlung is less self-absorbed in the anode; $\eta = 1$ therefore yields too hard a spectrum. With $\eta$ fixed at 0.40, all other configurations are predictions rather than fits.

{{REF:t3_1}} compares the two codes for 25–50 kV and 6–30° without the air path. With 1 mm Al, which removes the Cu K lines, the first HVL ({{REF:eq:hvl}}) of the DM is lower than that of SpekPy by 3.5–4.6 %. Without added filtration the HVL deviation ranges from −2.6 % to −19.4 %, because the DM underestimates the Cu K lines by about 25–43 %, and a weaker 8 keV line gives more weight to the softer 3–8 keV continuum in the air kerma. The deficit is largest at low voltage, where the Gryziński cross-section [@gryzinski1965] rises too slowly above the ionisation threshold compared with the distorted-wave Born calculations [@bote2008; @llovet2014] underlying the K-line model of SpekPy [@omar2018].

{{TABLE:t3_1}}

The spectral shapes of the two models agree within about 10 % between 10 and 30 keV ({{REF:fig05_validation_cu}}). The DM continuum is slightly richer in photons at 10–20 keV and poorer above about 25 keV, the ratio falling to 0.80–0.85 at 40 keV; this mild softening explains the HVL deficit of about 4 % for filtered beams. Its probable causes are the non-relativistic Born cross-section with the Elwert factor [@koch1959], which only approximates the tabulated cross-sections [@seltzer1986], and the assumed isotropic emission, whereas SpekPy models the angular distribution of bremsstrahlung [@omar2020a].

{{FIG:fig05_validation_cu|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (baseline geometry) from SpekPy v2 and the developed model without added filtration (left) and with 1.0 mm Al (right), normalised to equal bremsstrahlung area above 9.6 keV; lower panels: ratio of the two.}}

The absolute air kerma of the DM exceeds that of SpekPy by a factor of 1.08–1.22 without filtration and 1.21–1.42 with 1 mm Al, mainly because the DM neglects electron backscattering (the backscatter coefficient of copper is about 0.3). The DEF depends only on the spectral shape and is unaffected; absolute dose rates are taken from SpekPy. As a check beyond copper, the same $\eta$ was applied to a tungsten anode at 50, 80 and 100 kV with 2.5 mm Al, which removes the W L lines not included in the DM ({{REF:t3_1w}}). The HVL deviations lie between −5.7 % and +0.5 %, and at 50 kV both values are comparable to the first HVL (1.78 mm Al) of the quality RQR3 of the International Electrotechnical Commission standard IEC 61267 [@iec61267]. As for copper, the DM spectra have a slightly weaker high-energy tail ({{REF:fig06_validation_w}}). A single value of $\eta$ thus serves anodes with Z = 29 and Z = 74. The main weakness of the DM is the underestimated Cu K-line intensity, which matters for unfiltered and Kα-dominated beams, so the main DEF results use SpekPy spectra, with the DM as an independent check.

## 3.2. Spectrum of the 45 kV copper-anode tube

In the baseline spectrum ({{REF:fig07_baseline}}) the Be window and the air path remove practically all photons below about 3 keV. The continuum rises to a plateau at about 7–9 keV and drops abruptly at the Cu K-edge (8.98 keV), above which photons are absorbed in the anode much more strongly, so that the anode acts as its own K-edge filter. It then recovers to a shallow maximum near 12–13 keV and decreases to zero at 45 keV, so the Ag K-edge falls into its weak high-energy part. The Cu Kα (8.05 keV) and Kβ (8.91 keV) lines exceed the neighbouring continuum by more than two orders of magnitude.

{{FIG:fig07_baseline|Spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (θ = 12°, 0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air) from SpekPy v2 and the developed model. The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge (25.514 keV).}}

According to SpekPy, characteristic photons make up 68.5 % of the photon fluence ({{REF:t3_2}}), so the unfiltered beam is essentially a source of Cu K radiation. In the DM the line intensities follow from the K-shell ionisation yield (Section 2.4), the fluorescence yield $\omega_K = 0.4538$ and the radiative rates of xraylib [@schoonjans2011]. The Kβ/Kα fluence ratio of SpekPy, 0.1431, slightly exceeds the ratio of the radiative rates (0.134), because the more energetic Kβ photons are attenuated less on their way out. A 20 µm Ni filter, whose K-edge (8.333 keV, {{REF:t2_2}}) lies between the Kα and Kβ energies, reduces this ratio to 0.0023, acting as the classical β-filter of x-ray diffraction [@cullity2001].

The beam quality is very low: with a mean energy ({{REF:eq:emean}}) of 10.38 keV, a first HVL below 50 µm Al and an effective energy below the Kα energy, the dose to soft tissue is delivered mainly by the Cu K lines and the soft continuum. Only 4.64 % of the photons, carrying 14.1 % of the energy fluence, lie above the Ag K-edge, and their share in the kerma is smaller still because $\mu_{en}/\rho$ decreases steeply with energy. The output is very high: 383 mGy/mAs at 10 cm, i.e. 15.3 Gy/s at 40 mA. Combined with the dominance of 8 keV photons, for which $R(E)$ is only 21.0, this predicts a modest DEF for the bare beam.

## 3.3. Influence of tube design parameters

The anode angle, the tube voltage and the Be window thickness were varied one at a time around the baseline ({{REF:t3_3}}). Increasing the anode angle from 6° to 30° raises the air kerma by a factor of 1.56 because of weaker self-filtration in the anode: the exit path $d/\sin\theta$ of a photon produced at depth $d$ is about 4.8 times longer at 6° than at 30°. The effect is strongest where copper is most opaque, at the soft end and just above the Cu K-edge, whereas above about 25 keV the curves nearly merge ({{REF:fig08_angle}}). The same mechanism causes the heel effect [@bushberg2012], not treated here since the calculations refer to the central ray. A small anode angle thus gives a smaller effective focal spot but a lower output and a slightly harder beam, while the DEF hardly changes.

{{FIG:fig08_angle|Influence of the anode angle (6–30°) on the spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (0.5 mm Be, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The tube voltage has the strongest influence on the output: from 25 to 50 kV the air kerma increases from 163 to 430 mGy/mAs, largely through the Cu K lines, since the electrons travel a longer path at energies where the K-shell ionisation cross-section is near its maximum (at about 35 keV). The continuum grows strongly at high energies ({{REF:fig09_kvp}}) but hardly changes just above the Cu K-edge, because such photons escape only from a thin surface layer of the anode. The DEF increases only from 1.1932 at 25 kV to 1.2027 at 50 kV; at 25 kV no photon reaches the Ag K-edge, yet the DEF is already 1.19, so the enhancement of the unfiltered beam is caused almost entirely by photoabsorption in the L and M shells of Ag, where $R(E)$ is 21–32.

{{FIG:fig09_kvp|Spectra of the Cu-anode tube at tube voltages of 25–50 kV (baseline geometry; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The Be window affects only the soft end of the spectrum ({{REF:fig10_be}}). Because beryllium (Z = 4) absorbs weakly, increasing the window thickness from 0.15 to 2.0 mm removes mainly photons below about 6 keV and lowers the air kerma from 423 to 271 mGy/mAs, while the DEF rises only slightly. The thickness is fixed by the tube design but must be known accurately, since it changes the output by more than one third.

{{FIG:fig10_be|Influence of the Be window thickness (0.15–2.0 mm) on the low-energy part of the spectrum of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube (θ = 12°, 10 cm air; SpekPy v2).}}

The DM spectra give DEF values within 0.003 of SpekPy for all design variants. While the design parameters change the output by factors of up to 1.6 (anode angle, window) and 2.6 (voltage), the DEF for 1 % Ag remains between 1.19 and 1.21, because in every unfiltered beam the tissue dose is dominated by the Cu K lines and photons of 3–15 keV. The tube design is therefore a lever for the dose rate rather than for the DEF; a substantial increase in the DEF requires added filters.

## 3.4. Influence of added filters on the spectrum

Added filters reshape the spectrum through the transmission $\exp[-\mu_f(E)\,t]$ of {{REF:eq:filter}}, where $\mu_f(E)$ and $t$ are the linear attenuation coefficient and the thickness of the filter. Three groups were studied ({{REF:t3_4}}): aluminium, with its K-edge (1.56 keV) below the spectrum; nickel and copper, with K-edges near the Cu K lines; and Mo, Ag and Sn, with K-edges at 20.0, 25.514 and 29.2 keV ({{REF:t2_2}}). Aluminium acts as a classical hardening filter ({{REF:fig11_al}}): its photoelectric cross-section falls roughly as $E^{-3}$, so the soft continuum and the Cu K lines are removed far more efficiently than the photons above the Ag K-edge. With 1–2 mm Al the Kα line practically disappears and 49.8–65.0 % of the photons lie above the Ag K-edge. The HVL jumps from 0.104 to 0.454 mm Al between the 0.25 and 0.5 mm filters, because behind the thinner filter the residual Cu K lines still carry a large part of the air kerma. The price is the output: 1 mm Al reduces the dose rate about 120-fold and 2 mm Al about 270-fold.

{{FIG:fig11_al|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube with added Al filters of 0.25–2.0 mm (baseline geometry; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

Copper filters behave differently ({{REF:fig12_cu_ni}}). The Cu K-edge (8.979 keV) lies just above the Cu Kα and Kβ energies, so copper is relatively transparent to its own K radiation while absorbing the 9–20 keV continuum very strongly. With 0.1 mm Cu the mean energy is close to that with 0.5 mm Al, yet the HVL is 6.5 times smaller: the mean energy is weighted by fluence and reflects the hard continuum, whereas the HVL is weighted by air kerma and is controlled by the transmitted Kα line, for which $\mu_{en}/\rho$ of air is much larger. Only 0.2 mm Cu suppresses the lines sufficiently, at a relative air kerma of only $7.64 \times 10^{-4}$. A 0.02 mm Ni filter, with its K-edge between Kα and Kβ, removes Kβ and the continuum just above 8.33 keV while transmitting Kα; the resulting quasi-monochromatic 8 keV beam suits x-ray diffraction but not dose enhancement by Ag.

{{FIG:fig12_cu_ni|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube with a 0.02 mm Ni filter and with Cu filters of 0.05–0.2 mm (baseline geometry; SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

Each K-edge filter transmits a band below its edge and cuts the fluence above it several-fold or more ({{REF:fig13_kedge}}). The 0.05 mm Mo filter thus leaves only 31.5 % of its photons above the Ag K-edge, and the 0.05 mm Ag filter removes precisely the photons above 25.5 keV that are most useful for enhancement by Ag: although its HVL exceeds that of 0.5 mm Al, only 20.1 % of its photons lie above the Ag K-edge, against 38.2 %. Tin preserves the 25.5–29.2 keV window but removes the photons above 29.2 keV, where $R(E)$ is highest, so 0.1 mm Sn gives a slightly larger HVL than 1 mm Al but fewer photons above the Ag K-edge (40.4 % against 49.8 %). Neither the mean energy nor the HVL alone therefore characterises the part of the spectrum that matters for Ag. The DEF follows the fraction of photons above the Ag K-edge only while the kerma is not dominated by the Cu K lines: with 55.1 % of its photons above the edge, 0.1 mm Cu gives a DEF of only 1.288, because the transmitted Kα line delivers most of the tissue dose.

{{FIG:fig13_kedge|Spectra of the 45 kV Cu-anode tube filtered by Mo 0.05 mm, Ag 0.05 mm and Sn 0.05 and 0.1 mm compared with 1.0 mm Al, relative to the maximum of the Al-filtered spectrum (SpekPy v2). The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

## 3.5. Dose enhancement factor of Ag-loaded soft tissue

In the kerma approximation the DEF is a ratio of two spectrum-weighted integrals ({{REF:eq:def_spec}}), determined by the energy distribution of the dose integrand $E\,\Phi(E)\,\mu_{en}/\rho$ ({{REF:fig14_integrand}}). Without filtration the integrand is dominated by the Cu K lines and the 3–15 keV continuum and falls below $10^{-4}$ of its maximum above about 20 keV, because $\mu_{en}/\rho$ of tissue is so small there; the photons above the Ag K-edge therefore contribute almost nothing to the dose. In the dominant region $R(E)$ is only 21.0 at 8 keV and 26.9 at 15 keV, so 1 % Ag raises the integrand almost uniformly by about 20 %. With 1 mm Al the integrand is concentrated between 15 and 45 keV, and above the Ag K-edge, where the integrand of the loaded tissue jumps, $R(E)$ increases from 76.2 at 25.6 keV to 138.8 at 45 keV. Since about half of the photons of this beam lie above the edge, this region provides the larger part of the enhancement.

{{FIG:fig14_integrand|Dose integrand $E\,\Phi(E)\,\mu_{en}/\rho$ for ICRU-44 soft tissue (shaded) and tissue with 1 % Ag (line), without filtration (left) and with 1.0 mm Al (right); 45 kV Cu-anode tube, SpekPy v2. The dashed line marks the Ag K-edge.}}

The DEF increases linearly with the Ag mass fraction $w$ for every filter ({{REF:fig15_def_conc}}, {{REF:t3_5}}), as follows from the mixture rule ({{REF:eq:def_lin}}): $\mathrm{DEF} = 1 + w(\langle R\rangle - 1)$, where $\langle R\rangle$ is the mean of $R(E)$ weighted by the dose spectrum of unloaded tissue ({{REF:eq:ratio_avg}}). The slope per 1 % Ag grows from 0.202 without a filter to 0.804 with 0.2 mm Cu, i.e. $\langle R\rangle$ from about 21 to 81. For 1 % Ag the DEF is 1.20 without a filter, 1.48 with 1 mm Al, 1.62 with 2 mm Al and 1.80 with 0.2 mm Cu, whereas at 0.1 % Ag (1 mg of Ag per gram of tissue) the enhancement is only 2–8 %. The ranking of the filters ({{REF:t3_4}}) confirms that the DEF is governed by the share of the dose integrand above the Ag K-edge rather than by the mean energy: the nearly pure Cu Kα beam behind the Ni filter gives practically the same DEF as the unfiltered beam (1.208 against 1.202), and at a similar HVL 0.1 mm Sn yields less than 1 mm Al (1.444 against 1.482) because it removes the hardest photons. Strict linearity holds at the surface; inside an extended Ag-loaded region the attenuation by Ag itself makes the dependence non-linear (Section 3.7).

{{FIG:fig15_def_conc|Dose enhancement factor of ICRU-44 soft tissue versus the Ag mass fraction for different added filters (SpekPy v2 spectra, kerma approximation at the surface).}}

Compared with SpekPy, the DM gives a DEF lower by 0.6–1.3 % with 0.5–2 mm Al and by less than 1 % with the Sn, Ag and Mo filters, consistent with its weaker high-energy tail. Where residual Cu K lines carry much of the kerma, it is higher, by 0.5 % for 0.25 mm Al and about 2 % for 0.1 and 0.2 mm Cu, because the underestimated lines shift the dose towards the hard continuum. Such differences are small compared with those between filters, so the DEF is robust to the choice of spectrum model. With water instead of ICRU-44 tissue as the reference medium, the DEF for 1 % Ag is up to 1.3 % higher for filtered beams (1.4965 with 1 mm Al and 1.8274 with 0.2 mm Cu). Although ICRU-44 tissue contains less oxygen than water, its 1.3 % by mass of Na, P, S, Cl and K raises its $\mu_{en}/\rho$ above that of water at 15–40 keV and so lowers the DEF; this must be considered when DEF values for water and tissue are compared.

## 3.6. Optimisation of the filtration

Every filter raises the DEF at the expense of the dose rate, so the optimal filter maximises the DEF at a given transmission. In {{REF:fig16_tradeoff}} (xraylib attenuation coefficients applied to the unfiltered SpekPy spectrum) the Al and Ti curves practically coincide and lie highest from 1 down to about $10^{-3}$, whereas Ni and Cu stay low while the Cu Kα line is transmitted. The K-edge filters fall behind: Sn lies below Al and Ti at all transmissions, Mo levels off near 1.4, and Ag passes through a maximum of about 1.35.

{{FIG:fig16_tradeoff|Dose enhancement factor for 1 % Ag versus the relative air kerma for Al, Ti, Ni, Cu, Mo, Ag and Sn filters of increasing thickness (45 kV Cu-anode tube, baseline geometry; SpekPy v2).}}

For a systematic comparison, the thickness $t$ of each filter was found from the condition of equal air-kerma transmission

$$
\mathcal{T}_K(t) = \frac{\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi_0(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_{\mathrm{air}}\exp\left[-\mu_f(E)\,t\right]\mathrm{d}E}{\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi_0(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_{\mathrm{air}}\,\mathrm{d}E}
$$ {#eq:transm}

where $\mathcal{T}_K(t)$ is the air-kerma transmission, $\Phi_0(E)$ the fluence spectrum of the unfiltered beam, $(\mu_{en}/\rho)_{\mathrm{air}}$ the mass energy-absorption coefficient of air, $\mu_f(E)$ the linear attenuation coefficient of the filter element and $T_0$ = 45 keV the maximum photon energy. Equation {{REF:eq:transm}} was solved for $\mathcal{T}_K$ = 10 %, 1 % and 0.1 % for the elements from Al (Z = 13) to Bi (Z = 83), excluding gases, liquids, P, S, Se, the alkali metals, radioactive elements, Tl and the lanthanides other than Gd ({{REF:fig17_filter_scan}}; selected elements in {{REF:t3_6}}).

The dependence on Z reflects the positions of the absorption edges relative to the Cu lines and the Ag K-edge; at 10 % transmission all elements still give 1.220–1.241. Elements from Al to Co (Z = 13–27), with K-edges below the Cu Kα energy, absorb the Cu lines strongly and give 1.88–1.91 at 0.1 %, whereas for Ni to As (Z = 28–33) the K-edge lies above Kα, the transmitted line dominates the kerma, and these are the worst filters at 10 % and 1 %. Elements from Sr to Ag (Z = 38–47) absorb the photons above the Ag K-edge and form a deep minimum at 0.1 %, lowest for Ag, Pd and Rh (1.335–1.346); for Cd to Ba (Z = 48–56), with K-edges above the Ag K-edge, the DEF grows with Z. Heavier elements act through L-shell absorption: Gd, with its L3 edge (7.243 keV) below Kα, behaves like the light absorbers, whereas for Hf to Bi the L3 edges lie above Kα (11.919 keV for Au, {{REF:t2_2}}) and W, Au and Pb give only 1.350–1.351 at 1 %.

{{FIG:fig17_filter_scan|Dose enhancement factor for 1 % Ag with filters of elements Z = 13–83 whose thickness gives an equal air-kerma transmission of 10 %, 1 % and 0.1 % (45 kV Cu-anode tube, baseline geometry; SpekPy v2).}}

Among the light and medium-Z absorbers, Al, Si, Ca, Sc and Ti give practically the same DEF at 10 % and 1 % transmission, whereas at 0.1 % Ca, Sc, Ti and V give 1.907 and Al 1.884, i.e. 1.2 % less. As calcium and scandium are unsuitable as filter foils, Al and Ti are the practical materials. Titanium needs a much thinner foil (111.2 instead of 824.4 µm at 1 %) and is slightly more effective at strong attenuation, because in the several-millimetre-thick Al filter the weakly energy-dependent Compton scattering contributes more to the attenuation of the hard photons.

For an irradiation experiment, {{REF:t3_7}} gives the tissue kerma at the sample surface and the time needed to deliver a dose $D$ at tube current $I$,

$$
t_D = \frac{D}{c\,I\int_{0}^{T_0} \Phi(E)\,E\,\left(\mu_{en}/\rho\right)_t\,\mathrm{d}E}
$$ {#eq:texp}

where $D$ = 2 Gy, $I$ = 40 mA, $\Phi(E)$ is the fluence spectrum per unit tube charge at 10 cm (cm$^{-2}$ keV$^{-1}$ mAs$^{-1}$), $(\mu_{en}/\rho)_t$ refers to ICRU-44 tissue and $c = 1.602 \times 10^{-13}$ Gy g keV$^{-1}$ converts keV/g into Gy; under CPE this kerma equals the absorbed dose at the surface, without backscatter (Section 2.5). Without a filter 2 Gy are delivered in 0.12 s, which is difficult to time reproducibly, whereas 1 mm and 2 mm Al need 14.6 and 32.5 s. The 0.2 mm Cu filter owes the highest DEF of the studied set (1.80) only to its very strong attenuation (at an equal transmission of 0.1 %, Cu gives 1.74, Al and Ti 1.88–1.91); it needs 158.4 s, and its DEF changes steeply with thickness ({{REF:fig16_tradeoff}}), which makes it sensitive to tolerances.

{{TABLE:t3_7}}

Filtration is thus the decisive design lever for dose enhancement by Ag with a copper-anode tube. Aluminium of 1–2 mm, which raises the DEF for 1 % Ag from 1.20 to 1.48–1.62 at convenient exposure times, or titanium of 0.1–0.3 mm is the optimal choice. K-edge filters with edges at 20–30 keV (Mo, Ag, Sn) and filters that transmit the Cu Kα line (Ni, thin Cu) should be avoided.
