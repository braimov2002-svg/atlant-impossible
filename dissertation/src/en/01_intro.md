# INTRODUCTION

**Relevance of the topic.** Cancer is a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [@bray2024], and about half of cancer patients have an indication for radiotherapy [@delaney2005], whose effectiveness is limited by the tolerance of healthy tissues [@hall2018; @baskar2012]. One way to widen the therapeutic window is to use radiosensitizers [@wardman2007], among which compounds and nanoparticles of heavy metals are now studied intensively [@gill2019; @kuncic2018]. Elements of high atomic number $Z$ absorb kilovoltage photons far more strongly than soft tissue owing to the photoelectric effect [@podgorsak2016]. Gold nanoparticles improved the survival of tumour-bearing mice irradiated with 250 kVp (kilovolt peak) x-rays [@hainfeld2004], and dose enhancement by gold is much larger for kilovoltage than for megavoltage beams [@cho2005]. Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) were proposed as a radiosensitizer for glioma [@liu2013] and outperformed gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) in vitro and in mice [@liu2016]; silver is also cheaper, and its K-edge at 25.514 keV lies inside the spectrum of a 45 kV x-ray tube.

The physical effect is quantified by the dose enhancement factor (DEF), the ratio of the absorbed doses in tissue with and without silver. The ratio of the mass energy-absorption coefficients of silver and soft tissue is only 21–32 at 8–25 keV, about 76 at the Ag K-edge and 100–139 at 30–45 keV, so the DEF depends on the fraction of the photon energy fluence above the edge [@cho2005; @roeske2007]. The tube voltage and half-value layer (HVL) that usually specify kilovoltage beams [@ma2001] do not fix the spectrum around the edge, which must therefore be modelled [@poludniowski2021]. Unlike tungsten-anode tubes [@bushberg2012; @khan2014], a 45 kV copper-anode tube emits most photons of the unfiltered beam in the Cu K lines at 8.0–8.9 keV, far below the Ag K-edge, and its spectrum, hence the DEF, depends on the anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window and filters, which makes its modelling for DEF calculations a relevant problem of medical physics.

**Degree of study of the problem.** Dose enhancement near high-Z media goes back to Spiers's analysis of bone irradiated with kilovoltage x-rays [@spiers1949]. Enhancement by iodine was calculated for kilovoltage beams [@mello1983; @solberg1992] and used to treat brain metastases with a modified computed tomography scanner [@rose1999]. Gold nanoparticles have been studied extensively in cells and by Monte Carlo (MC) simulation [@chithrani2010; @rahman2009; @zhang2009; @leung2011], their mechanisms reviewed [@butterworth2012; @her2017; @schuemann2016] and elements compared as functions of photon energy [@roeske2007; @mcmahon2016]; the enhancement around nanoparticles is highly localised [@jones2010], and such nanoscale deposition can explain biological effects beyond the macroscopic dose enhancement [@mcmahon2011]. For silver, radiosensitization by AgNPs was shown in cancer cells [@xu2009], glioma [@liu2018; @zhao2019], breast cancer cells [@swanner2015] and colorectal cancer [@habiba2019]. AgNPs are themselves cytotoxic and induce reactive oxygen species and DNA damage [@asharani2009], so part of their effect may be chemical or biological. Dosimetric studies include MC calculations for brain-tumour brachytherapy [@taha2019], healthy tissues around a loaded tumour [@caglar2024] and layered skin [@esitmez2026], dose-gel measurements [@merkis2023] and an MC phantom study of depth dependence in orthovoltage beams [@chow2023].

Spectrum models range from empirical [@boone1997] and semi-empirical [@birch1979; @tucker1991] to deterministic physics-based [@poludniowski2007a; @poludniowski2007b; @poludniowski2009] and MC models [@verhaegen1999; @ay2004], mostly for tungsten anodes; SpekPy, validated against measurements [@bujila2020], supports a copper anode in version 2 [@poludniowski2021]. Copper-anode tubes serve mainly x-ray diffraction, with a nickel filter isolating Cu Kα [@cullity2001], whereas silver needs photons above its K-edge. The relation between the design and filtration of such a tube, its spectrum and the DEF of silver-loaded tissue has therefore not been quantified in the literature reviewed, which determined the aim of this work.

**Aim of the work.** The aim is to model the spectrum of a 45 kV copper-anode x-ray tube as a function of its design parameters and filtration and, on this basis, to determine the DEF of silver-containing soft tissue and the filtration that maximises it at an acceptable dose rate.

**Tasks of the research.** The following tasks were solved:

1. To review the literature on radiosensitization by high-Z elements, especially silver, and on spectrum modelling.
2. To develop a semi-analytical thick-target spectrum model of a copper-anode tube including electron slowing-down, bremsstrahlung, direct and bremsstrahlung-induced K radiation and attenuation in the anode, window, air and filters.
3. To validate the model against SpekPy v2 and fit its single parameter.
4. To calculate the spectrum and beam quality versus anode angle, tube voltage, beryllium window and filters.
5. To calculate the DEF for 0.1–2 % silver by mass and find the filters that maximise it at an acceptable dose rate.
6. To study the depth dependence of the DEF and self-shielding and verify them by an independent MC calculation.
7. To compare silver with other high-Z elements, estimate the DEF uncertainty and formulate recommendations for experiments.

**Object of the research.** The object is the x-ray radiation of a 45 kV copper-anode tube and its interaction with soft tissue containing silver.

**Subject of the research.** The subject is the formation of the spectrum, its dependence on tube design and filtration, and the resulting DEF.

**Research methods.** The spectrum is calculated with the developed model (DM), a semi-analytical thick-target model based on the Bethe stopping power, the non-relativistic Bethe–Heitler cross-section with the Elwert factor and the Gryzinski K-shell cross-section [@bethe1930; @bethe1934; @elwert1939; @koch1959; @gryzinski1965], with SpekPy v2.5.4 as the reference [@spekpy_soft]. Interaction data are from the NIST tables [@nist_xraymass] and xraylib [@schoonjans2011], with ICRU-44 soft tissue as the reference medium [@icru44]. The DEF is calculated in the kerma approximation [@attix1986] and verified by an MC photon-transport code written for this work, with Woodcock tracking [@woodcock1965], Ag K fluorescence, coherent and incoherent scattering [@hubbell1975] and local electron energy deposition. Uncertainties are estimated by sensitivity analysis; the code uses Python with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib [@harris2020; @virtanen2020; @hunter2007].

**Scientific novelty.** The novelty of the work is as follows:

1. A semi-analytical spectrum model of a reflection-type copper-anode tube has been developed with a single adjustable parameter, the electron depth factor $\eta$, and a closed-form description of bremsstrahlung-induced K radiation. With $\eta = 0.40$ (fitted to the SpekPy v2 bremsstrahlung shape, root-mean-square deviation 2.46 %), the DM reproduces without refitting the SpekPy HVL for 1 mm Al within −3.5 to −4.6 % and the mean energy within 0.54 keV at 25–50 kV and anode angles of 6–30°.
2. Without added filtration, the DEF for 1 % silver by mass is only 1.20 (1.19–1.21 for anode angles of 6–30°, 25–50 kV and beryllium windows of 0.15–2 mm), because the dose is dominated by the Cu K lines and soft bremsstrahlung. Filtration is decisive: the DEF is 1.48 with 1 mm Al, 1.62 with 2 mm Al and 1.80 with 0.2 mm Cu, at the cost of a 120–1300-fold lower air kerma.
3. At equal air-kerma transmission, light and medium-Z filters without absorption edges at 9–45 keV (Al, Si, Ca, Sc, Ti) give the highest DEF and filters with K-edges at 20–30 keV (Mo, Ag, Sn) the lowest under strong filtration: at 0.1 % transmission, the DEF for 1 % silver is about 1.91 with Sc or Ti but 1.34 with Ag.
4. With 1 mm Al, the DEF for 1 % silver rises from 1.48 at the surface to 1.69 at 20 mm depth (beam hardening) but falls to 1.21 at 10 mm inside a uniformly loaded region (self-shielding); the dose behind a 10 mm layer with 1 % silver drops to about 0.80 of the silver-free value. An independent MC calculation confirmed these results within 1–10 % and showed the role of reabsorbed Ag K fluorescence and scattered photons.
5. Per unit mass, silver is a weaker physical sensitizer at 45 kV than gold, platinum, bismuth and gadolinium (DEF 1.48 against 2.16 for gold, 1 mm Al) and comparable to iodine (1.46); the stronger biological effect reported for AgNPs than for AuNPs [@liu2016] therefore points to chemical, biological and nanoscale mechanisms.

**Practical significance.** The DM software provides the spectrum, air kerma and beam quality of low-voltage copper-anode tubes for planning radiobiological experiments with silver-containing radiosensitizers. For a 45 kV tube, a 1–2 mm aluminium filter (or 0.1–0.3 mm titanium) is recommended; with aluminium, a tissue kerma of 2 Gy at the sample surface (10 cm from the focal spot, 40 mA) takes about 15–33 s. The CS_Energy function of xraylib 4.3.0 underestimates the mass energy-absorption coefficients of light elements at 15–45 keV by up to 37 % (oxygen at 30 keV), which would overestimate the DEF (1.72 instead of 1.48 for 1 % silver and 1 mm Al), so the NIST tables should be used.

**Reliability of the results.** Reliability rests on evaluated data (NIST photon interaction tables [@nist_xraymass], ICRU Report 37 stopping-power parameters [@icru37]) and independent checks. The Bethe formula reproduces the NIST ESTAR collision stopping power of water at 50 keV (6.603 MeV cm$^2$/g) [@nist_estar], and the DM reproduces the SpekPy HVL for a tungsten anode at 50–100 kV within 6 %, SpekPy itself being validated against measurements [@bujila2020]. The MC energy-transfer coefficients reproduce the NIST mass energy-absorption coefficients of H, C and O at 20–40 keV within 0.3 %. The sensitivity analysis gives DEF = 1.48 ± 0.02 (coverage factor $k = 1$) for 1 % silver and 1 mm Al.
